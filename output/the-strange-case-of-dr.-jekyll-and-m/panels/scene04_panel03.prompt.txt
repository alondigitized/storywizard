Close-up medium shot framed on a heavy Victorian door — dark mahogany, iron hardware, the threshold between Utterson and something unknowable. The door fills two-thirds of the frame, dominating the composition with its mass and silence. Camera is positioned slightly low, making the door loom.

Utterson stands to the left foreground, lean silhouette in conservative dark suit and muted grey-brown overcoat, profile turned toward the door. His deep-set eyes are wide, jaw locked. One gloved hand is raised mid-knock or frozen against the wood — suspended, uncertain. He is lit from the right by a single gaslight sconce just behind him, casting his long shadow diagonally across the floorboards toward the door. His color associations: weathered grey-brown, reliable and muted against the dark corridor.

The door itself is the visual fulcrum. Light bleeds beneath it from within — but the light is wrong, divided: one half amber-gold (warm gaslight, Jekyll's warmth, civilized and rational) and one half a deep sickly crimson that bleeds at the edge (Hyde's presence, violence, the primal). The two colors meet and bleed into each other in the gap under the door, creating an unsettling gradient that pulses visually between warmth and threat. The keyhole breathes a sliver of crimson light.

The door's shadow on the wall beside it stretches long and distorted — subtly not quite the shape of a door. It twists at the top, organic, like something behind the wood is pressing outward against the grain.

Background: a narrow Victorian corridor, high wainscoting, faded wallpaper with a repeating fleur-de-lis pattern dissolving into charcoal shadow at the edges. The laboratory door in the far background (barely visible through the murk) lurks in total darkness. Gaslight sconces line the hall but the nearest ones are dim, their glass clouded. Fog curls in from an unseen source — low to the floor, creeping.

Art style: Mignola-influenced heavy black ink blocks with McKean textural wash layering — ink wash grain over the shadows, cross-hatching on the wood grain of the door, rough impasto texture on the wallpaper. No clean lines; everything bleeds slightly at the edges.

Color palette strictly: deep charcoal (#2C2C2C) for the corridor void, sickly amber (#D4A847) for Utterson's gaslit side, blood crimson (#8B0000) bleeding under the door, cold blue-grey (#6B7B8D) in the far shadows of the hall, ivory (#F5F0E1) in the crack of keyhole light at its most neutral point.

Mood: oppressive stillness before rupture. The silence rendered visible. The feeling that the door is listening back.
