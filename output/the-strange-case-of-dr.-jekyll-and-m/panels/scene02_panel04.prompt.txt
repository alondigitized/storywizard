IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme close-up portrait of Mr. Hyde's face filling the entire frame, cropped just below the chin and cutting off the top of his skull at the hairline. The focal point is his eyes — sharp, glittering, too alert, pupils unnervingly dilated, catching the amber gaslight like a cat's in the dark. His smile is the second focal point: stretched fractionally too wide across pale, bloodless skin, revealing teeth that are slightly too even, the expression containing no warmth whatsoever — the mechanical mimicry of joy.

Hyde is a small, pale man with a face that is not malformed but deeply, inexplicably wrong. His proportions are subtly off — the jaw slightly too narrow, the brow slightly too low, the eyes set at an angle that reads as predatory. The wrongness is felt before it is understood. His skin is waxy, sallow, rendered sickly under the amber gaslight that rakes across his face from below-left, carving deep shadows beneath his cheekbones and brow ridge. His hair is dark, lank, slightly disheveled. The heavy collar of Jekyll's oversized frock coat rises around his neck, swallowing his small frame.

Lighting is a single amber gaslight source from below, casting the sickly golden-amber glow of moral corruption across his face. The light intensifies the pallor of his skin, turning it a jaundiced ivory-yellow. Deep charcoal shadows pool in his eye sockets, beneath his nose, and in the corners of that too-wide smile. The background is pure darkness — impenetrable deep charcoal black, with the faintest suggestion of a Victorian fog-diffused gaslight halo behind him, dissolving into nothing.

Art style: Dark Victorian noir executed in heavy ink wash textures with aggressive crosshatching in the shadow regions. Influenced by Mike Mignola's brutal use of black mass and Dave McKean's unsettling portraiture — the face rendered with visceral detail at the eyes and mouth, dissolving into gestural ink wash at the edges. The line work is dense and scratchy, suggesting something organic and alive in the shadows. No clean outlines — edges bleed into darkness.

Color palette is almost monochromatic: deep charcoal blacks dominate the frame, the sickly amber gaslight is the only color, concentrated on his skin and eyes. A faint, barely perceptible blush of blood crimson haunts the inner corners of his eyes and the wet line of his lips — Hyde's color bleeding through. No blues, no whites. The ivory of his skin is jaundiced toward amber, corrupted.

Emotion: pure reptilian wrongness. The image should evoke the visceral, instinctive revulsion of encountering something that wears a human face but is not quite human — the uncanny valley rendered in gaslight and shadow. Dread that bypasses rational thought. The feeling of being watched by something that does not blink often enough.
