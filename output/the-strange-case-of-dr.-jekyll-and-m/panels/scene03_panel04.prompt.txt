IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme low-angle close-up shot, camera nearly flush with the wet cobblestones of a fog-drenched Victorian London street at night. The focal point is a shattered walking cane broken cleanly in two, the splintered halves splayed apart on the uneven stone pavement — the wood is dark, rare, dense, visibly stressed at the break point with white-fibered splinters exploding outward. The fracture radiates immense, insensate force.

Spreading from beneath the broken cane across the cobblestones is a wide, irregular pool that catches the cold blue-grey cast of moonlight — its surface ambiguous, viscous, neither fully silver nor fully crimson, bleeding into the gaps between stones with the slow certainty of something that cannot be undone. The liquid edges catch both moonlight (#6B7B8D blue-grey) and a distant gaslight's sickly amber (#D4A847), creating a sickening iridescence. Deep blood crimson (#8B0000) pulses in the shadows at the pool's heart.

Hyde is absent. His absence is the subject. The heavy fog fills the space where he stood — thick, roiling, almost physical, swallowing the mid-ground completely. Wisps curl around a distant iron lamppost, its gaslight a weak amber smear barely visible through the murk, casting almost no illumination. Narrow Victorian cobblestone alley walls rise on either side, streaked with damp, shrinking the frame into a claustrophobic corridor. In the far background, barely discernible through fog, the dark outline of a doorway — shadowed, looming, threshold between worlds.

The composition emphasizes emptiness and aftermath. No figures. No movement. Only the evidence of catastrophic violence rendered in stillness. The broken cane dominates the foreground, massive in frame against the tiny, retreating street. The fog acts as an erasure — Hyde dissolved into it, but his crimson presence bleeds through the stones.

Art style: Dark Victorian noir ink wash. Heavy Mignola-esque black masses dominate the frame — the cobblestones are suggested with angular ink lines, not rendered in detail. McKean-influenced texture overlays give the fog a collage-like, almost photographic grain. Deep charcoal (#2C2C2C) crushes the shadows to near-black. Ink wash bleeds at the edges of the pool, suggesting violence that cannot be contained. Cross-hatching on the cane's broken wood emphasizes texture and violence. The overall palette is nearly monochrome — charcoal, blue-grey, and amber — punctured by the blood crimson pooling beneath the broken cane. Shadows stretch long and distorted across the cobblestones, reaching toward the viewer. Mood: cold, irreversible, haunted. The fog does not comfort — it conceals.
