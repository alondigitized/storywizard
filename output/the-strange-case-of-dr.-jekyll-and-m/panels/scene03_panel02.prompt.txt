Diagonal split-panel composition: a single cobblestone street bisected by a hard diagonal line from upper-left to lower-right. The right half is consumed by encroaching blood crimson shadow (#8B0000), deep charcoal darkness bleeding across the diagonal like a wound spreading through fabric. Edward Hyde emerges from this darkness — small, dwarfish, unnervingly slight, wearing Jekyll's oversized clothes hanging loose on his narrow frame, a heavy cape swallowing his shoulders. He grips a thick walking cane in one pale hand, arm slightly raised, posture coiled with barely restrained violence. His face is angled downward toward Carew, wearing that unsettling smile — features not deformed, but fundamentally wrong in proportion, something insectile in the alertness of his stillness.

Hyde's shadow stretches far across the cobblestones, disproportionately massive — twice his body's height, reaching toward Carew like a predator's claw. The shadow bleeds crimson at its edges, darkening to charcoal at its core. Despite Hyde's small stature, everyone he stands near appears dwarfed in spirit even as they tower in height — Carew's silhouette taller by a full head, yet somehow already diminished by proximity.

Background: narrow Victorian London street, cramped Georgian facades pressing inward from both sides, gaslit amber (#D4A847) lamp visible on the far right casting a sickly halo that the crimson darkness actively consumes. A laboratory door — heavy, dark oak, iron-hinged — visible in the distant background shadow, deliberately ambiguous, always watching. Thick ground-level fog rolls across cobblestones, parting around Hyde's feet as if repelled.

Art style: Mike Mignola-influenced ink wash with Dave McKean collage energy — heavy black ink lines, dramatic negative space, texture in shadows achieved through layered cross-hatching and ink bleed. Moonlit half rendered in thin, delicate linework; Hyde's half in thick, aggressive brushstrokes. No smooth gradients — all tonal transitions are graphic and harsh. Ivory white (#F5F0E1) used sparingly for the moonlit cobblestones and Carew's coat, amplifying his vulnerability. The diagonal split itself rendered as a visible, almost physical boundary — a crack in reality.

Mood: predatory stillness one breath before catastrophe. The peaceful half already feels like memory. A sense of wrongness radiates from Hyde's half of the frame — not chaos yet, but inevitability. Oppressive Victorian dread, claustrophobic shadows alive with malice.
