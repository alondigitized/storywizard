Triptych composition, three vertical frames side by side within a single panel, each depicting the same narrow Victorian cobblestone alley at different times of day. The frames share a continuous background — the same recessed doorway, the same gas lamp post, the same damp stone walls — but the light and shadow shift dramatically across each frame.

**Left frame — Dawn:** Cold blue-grey light bleeds over fog-drenched cobblestones. Utterson stands motionless to the left of the doorway, a lean, long figure in a conservative dark suit and weathered overcoat, collar turned up against the chill. His deep-set eyes are fixed on the door. A single gas lamp still burns amber, fighting the pale morning. Fog pools at his feet. The street is deserted. His shadow stretches thin and long behind him.

**Center frame — Noon:** Flat, joyless grey light — no warmth, no color — washes the alley. Utterson occupies the same position, same posture, slightly more rigid. His face is turned three-quarters toward the door, jaw firm, watchful eyes patient and unblinking. A few distant pedestrian silhouettes blur in the background fog, but none approach the door. The gaslight is extinguished. Shadows retreat but never fully vanish — they pool in doorframes and under eaves.

**Right frame — Night:** Gaslight returns, casting a sickly amber corona on wet cobblestones. The door is swallowed in deep charcoal shadow. Utterson now stands closer, half-consumed by darkness himself, only the amber highlight of his rugged jaw and the gleam of his watchful eyes catching the light. Fog thickens into near-opacity. His shadow on the wall behind him is massive, distorted, doubling his silhouette like a second figure. The street is utterly empty. The recessed laboratory door looms at the edge of frame, a threshold into absolute blackness.

Across all three frames, the doorway anchors the right edge — identical, unchanging, perpetually in shadow. The repetition of Utterson's silhouette creates a sentinel rhythm. Heavy ink wash textures roughen the stone walls. Cross-hatched shadows deepen with each frame. Color palette: deep charcoal dominates, sickly amber gaslight punctuates the dawn and night frames, cold blue-grey drains the noon frame of life.

Art style: Dark Victorian noir, Mike Mignola-influenced heavy shadow blocking with Dave McKean ink wash texture. Thick expressive linework. Fog rendered as translucent layered washes. Atmosphere oppressive and claustrophobic — the street a cage Utterson willingly inhabits. Mood: obsessive vigilance, creeping dread, the weight of a secret not yet known but already felt.
