Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga.

TALL NARROW VERTICAL PANEL. Extreme intimate close-up: two faces nearly nose to nose, divided by the nursery windowsill that bisects the frame horizontally at its precise midpoint. The composition is a study in collision — two worlds pressed against the same glass threshold. Peter occupies the upper half of the frame, the open night sky behind him in deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) shot through with cold stars; Wendy occupies the lower half, wrapped in the warm Nursery Amber (#C8860A) of lamplight pooling from inside the room. The window frame itself — rendered in precise straight architectural lines appropriate to a London sequence — divides them like a panel border made physical. A subtle scale-pattern motif in the window's weathered wood grain: the Crocodile's shadow, an undertone only attentive eyes will find.

Peter's face is loose and alive, rendered in gestural watercolor strokes — his features caught mid-grin, chin slightly raised, his whole expression poised at the edge of a crow. His hair lifts toward the top of the frame in a night wind that does not reach the room below. The mark-making on him is deliberately freer, slightly blurred at the edges, as though he is already partly somewhere else and the image cannot quite pin him down. His shadow — where it should fall across the window frame — is absent or displaced, misaligned from the light source. Viridian (#1A7A5E) green glows softly in the night sky behind him.

Wendy's face below is rendered with Dulac-precision: every line deliberate, every shadow placed with care. She is grown — young adulthood settling into her features with quiet permanence. Her expression holds the almost-smile: the corners of her mouth carry warmth, but her bright eyes hold something older, a grief without a name yet, the first full understanding arriving just now and not yet survivable. Her Wendy Periwinkle (#7B9DC4) palette cool and steady; London Slate (#6B7280) entering the tones of her dress collar and cheek shadow. Her face is perfectly still. She is not moving toward him or away.

The gap between his grin and her almost-smile — that millimeter of air between their noses — is the emotional center of the panel. His wind does not touch her hair.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy — he is never quite still. His face is beautiful and slightly alien, large luminous eyes without visible pupils in close-up — beautiful but inhuman, the visual shorthand for his apartness from ordinary childhood. A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, and umber browns. No shadow, or shadow behaving as an independent entity. The crow gesture encoded in his raised chin and the edge-of-grin that never quite resolves into grief.
