IMAGE PROMPT:
Tall, narrow vertical panel split by the window frame like a hairline fracture between two worlds. An intimate close-up: two faces nearly nose-to-nose, the wooden windowsill bisecting the composition horizontally at mid-panel. Peter occupies the upper half — framed behind him, open Midnight Blue night sky pricked with silver stars, a wind moving through his hair that belongs entirely to him, to the second star and somewhere beyond. His face is alight with effortless, careless confidence — a wide grin, chin slightly lifted, luminous pupil-less eyes already reading some invisible horizon. His hair lifts and drifts rightward in a gust she cannot feel. His leaf-tunic catches no lamplight. He is rendered with loose, gestural mark-making — ink lines that cannot quite settle, as if he resists being held still on the page. Wendy fills the lower half — behind her, warm Nursery Amber lamplight pools gold across the interior, catching the side of her face, illuminating her with the glow of a room she has grown almost too large for. Her face is rendered in Dulac-style precision: every line deliberate, every shadow architectural, the emerging adulthood of her features drawn with controlled crosshatching that makes her feel undeniably, painfully real. Her eyes are bright — luminous with something she cannot yet name. Her mouth is almost a smile, but the muscles around it carry the first, barely-formed weight of grief. She is not crying. She is not certain. She is simply beginning to understand something enormous and gentle and irreversible. The distance between his grin and her almost-smile is the full width of the tragedy. The window frame between them is the threshold of worlds — London's precise architectural linework on her side, the slightly organic, irregular border of Neverland beginning on his. A suggestion of Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) in the fine hatching of the window frame itself — a discovered detail, never labored. No wind touches Wendy's hair. The Nursery Amber on her side does not reach him.
