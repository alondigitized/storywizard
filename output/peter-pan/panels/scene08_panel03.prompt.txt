TALL NARROW VERTICAL PANEL — extreme close-up of two faces nearly nose-to-nose within a window threshold, the frame split horizontally at the glazing bar: Peter in the upper half against open night sky, Wendy in the lower half framed by warm lamplight. The composition maps two color worlds pressed together — deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) and Fairy Dust Silver above, Nursery Amber (#C8860A) gold below — bleeding precisely at the window sill, neither world yielding.

Peter's face dominates the upper frame: wide grin, chin lifted in easy confidence, luminous eyes catching starlight from the sky behind him. He is already half in another adventure — his gaze drifts past her shoulder, not cruel, simply elsewhere. His hair lifts in a wind she cannot feel, the invisible current of Neverland's permanent beyond stirring his dark locks away from his forehead. The linework on him is loose and gestural — slightly blurred at his edges, ink resisting his stillness. Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) bleeds in softly at the upper corners.

Wendy's face fills the lower frame with Dulac-precision — every plane of her emerging cheekbone and jaw rendered with deliberate, careful line. She is almost smiling. The almost is everything. Her eyes are bright, genuinely lit by the amber lamplight pooling across her cheekbones — but her mouth holds something unresolved, the first barely-formed understanding of a grief she cannot yet name. She is looking directly at him. He is not quite looking back. Her Wendy Periwinkle (#7B9DC4) palette holds cool and steady against the warmth behind her — a dependable sky pressed to the glass. Her white nightgown collar shows a faint grass stain at its edge; a small thimble glints at her throat.

The Edwardian glazing bar bisects the panel as the story's essential geometry — two worlds divided by the width of a window frame. The distance between his grin and her almost-smile is rendered in two inches of vertical space, and it is the whole tragedy.
