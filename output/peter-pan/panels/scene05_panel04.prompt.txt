IMAGE PROMPT:
Wide, extreme low-angle shot from the forest floor — the viewer crouches among roots and fallen leaves in the undergrowth, looking upward through tangled undergrowth at a torchlit procession moving between massive tree trunks. The composition is formally symmetrical and slightly oppressive, the canopy above swallowing all light beyond the torches' reach.

Four scarred, tattooed ruffians haul a low curtained chariot by thick ropes, their bodies angled with the effort, faces twisted into leering, wary grimaces — eyes flicking sideways into the black between trees. Their skin is weathered and marked, their silhouettes grotesque against the amber torch-wash. Behind them, a chorus of pirate shadow-faces recedes into near-total darkness — shapes more than men, suggested by crosshatching and glints of torchlight on blades and gold teeth.

At the center, reclining on the curtained chariot with absolute, unhurried magnificence: HOOK. His deep crimson velvet coat spreads like a king's robe, its tarnished gold brocade catching the torch-amber in fractured gleams. Long jet-black ringlets cascade over his shoulders, framing a cadaverous jaw and a face of courteous, tranquil threat. Two cigars smoulder simultaneously at the corner of his mouth, their twin curls of smoke drifting organically upward into the panel's dark border. His iron hook rests across one knee — rendered with precise, deliberate clarity, every curve and taper exactly inked, the single most sharply defined object in the panel, catching torchlight like a cold star amid the soft amber chaos around it. His expression reveals nothing. He looks not like a man moving toward battle, but like a sovereign being carried toward an entertainment he himself arranged.

Alongside the chariot, SMEE waddles with cheerful purposefulness — compact, ruddy-cheeked, old-fashioned spectacles perched on his broad nose, striped knit cap, loose work shirt. A short blade hangs at his belt with the casual domesticity of a pocket watch. His apple-cheeked pleasantness reads as genuinely vertiginous in this context — a shopkeeper in a procession of nightmares.

Dominant light source: four amber-gold torches carried at procession-flanks, casting hard upward shadows on all faces, turning the tree trunks into cathedral columns of warm light and dense shadow. Beyond the torch-radius, everything resolves to Midnight Blue-black. Hook's iron hook blazes against the shadow. His disproportionately large shadow stretches upward and back, belonging to myth rather than physics.

Organic, slightly irregular Neverland panel borders. The forest floor debris — roots, leaves, a single suggestion of crocodile-scale texture in the bark hatching of the nearest tree — frames the bottom of the composition. The procession recedes into threatening darkness at panel-right.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Smee: Hook's Irish bosun — compact, ruddy-faced, apple-cheeked, small bright eyes behind old-fashioned spectacles, striped knit cap, loose work shirt, heavy canvas trousers, practical boots, amiable pleasantness that never varies; color palette: London Slate and Lost Boy Earth.

Art style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac linework, organic and gestural in Neverland, rich ink-heavy shadows built with crosshatching, warm golden magical light sources, dark fairy tale aesthetic, oversaturated cherished-memory quality throughout.
