IMAGE PROMPT:
Medium close-up, intimate. The Darling nursery at night, the world narrowed to a single gas lamp's amber radius — all else soft, ink-rich shadow. The lamp burns off-frame right, throwing Nursery Amber (#C8860A) across Wendy's shoulder, her white nightgown, and the single bright point of a needle mid-thread.

Wendy perches on the bed's edge, elevated slightly above Peter in the composition — unhurried, unflappable, faintly amused. Her face turns toward her work; her hands are in purposeful motion, one holding Peter's detached shadow carefully at his ankle, the other drawing thread through with practiced ease. The needle catches the lamplight as one precise, brilliant point against the surrounding dark.

Peter sits cross-legged on the floorboards directly before her, one bare foot extended, his shadow laid carefully at his ankle awaiting repair. His face tilts up toward Wendy — an expression caught between brave and apprehensive, luminous pupilless eyes wide, watching her with the unguarded uncertainty of someone who has never once needed anyone's help before and finds it entirely foreign territory.

In the upper corner of the panel, barely larger than a clenched fist, Tinker Bell hovers as a fierce incandescent silhouette — arms crossed, whole body tilted at a furious angular pitch. Her light bleeds hot amber into crimson at its edges, the color of consuming jealousy. The nearest ceiling corner receives a secondary ruddy-gold cast distinct from the gas lamp's warmer amber below — her emotional state visible in the color of the shadows she throws.

The linework around Wendy and the nursery interior is controlled and architectural — precise crosshatching, straight-edged shadows, London's ordered domestic visual grammar. Tinker Bell's glow has softer, more organic edges pressing against that geometry.
