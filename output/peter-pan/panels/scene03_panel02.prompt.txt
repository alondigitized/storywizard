IMAGE PROMPT:
Medium close-up, warm and intimate. The nursery is reduced to a single island of amber light — one gas lamp burning low, its warm gold cone illuminating Wendy seated on the edge of her bed and Peter cross-legged on the floor before her, the surrounding room dissolving into soft, velvety darkness. The viewpoint is level, just slightly below Wendy's eyeline, granting Peter's upturned face a trace of that mythic scale. Wendy holds a threaded needle at the moment before stitching — the needle itself catches the lamplight as a single brilliant white point, the compositional focal pin of the entire panel. Her posture is erect and untroubled, the posture of a girl for whom competence is simply the baseline. Her expression is open, faintly amused, kind without condescension. One bare foot of Peter's is extended toward her, his detached shadow laid carefully across his ankles like a dark ribbon of cloth awaiting the needle. Peter's face tips upward toward Wendy, caught between brave composure and a visible, carefully contained apprehension — he is trying very hard to look as though this does not concern him at all. In the upper corner of the panel, small as a clenched fist, Tinker Bell hovers — a fierce incandescent silhouette, her arms crossed tightly, her entire tiny body tilted forward in the body language of furious judgment. Her light where it touches the upper wall pulses hot amber-red rather than clear gold, the jealous frequency. The linework around Wendy is controlled, architectural, precise; Tinker Bell's glow spreads with softer, more organic, flame-like edges. No shadows fall correctly near Tinker Bell — her fury bleeds warmth in all the wrong directions. The gas lamp casts Nursery Amber (#C8860A) across everything — the white cotton of Wendy's nightgown, the wooden bed frame, the floorboards — while the detached shadow reads as a flat, slightly iridescent near-black against the warm planking.
