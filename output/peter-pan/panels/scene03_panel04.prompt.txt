IMAGE PROMPT:
Shallow wide-ratio horizontal panel, low ceiling pressing down conspiratorially, warm Nursery Amber gaslight dominating the left two-thirds. The foot of Wendy's bed is the central stage: Peter lies across it on both elbows, body angled toward camera-left, face brought close to Wendy's with the precise, calculated closeness of a boy who knows exactly what he is doing. His expression is luminous and slightly predatory in its charm — the grin of something older than it appears. Wendy faces him on her side, chin resting in both cupped hands, leaning imperceptibly forward, her posture the physical shape of a decision already made without her knowledge. The gas lamp at bedside casts deep Nursery Amber warmth across both faces, crosshatched shadows pooling beneath the low ceiling and under the bedframe. Just right of center, John has pushed himself upright against his headboard, nightcap tilted rakishly sideways, a forgotten book lying open and face-down on the blanket — he peers toward the conversation with barely suppressed hunger, spectacles slightly skewed. At the far right, barely distinguishable in the shadow of a half-drawn bed curtain, Michael's face appears as nothing more than two wide eyes and a small nose — a child watching something wonderful through a crack in the world. Near Peter's left shoulder, Tinker Bell hovers as a single tight, cool point of light — distinctly colder and smaller than the amber gaslight, a silver-white spark that casts no warmth on the surrounding fabric, conspicuously apart from the room's gold. The nursery window behind the group is a dark rectangle against which London's night presses heavily, the glass barely catching the gas lamp's amber in a thin rim of light — the world outside indifferent and close.
