Extreme close-up diptych composition, the panel bisected by a hard diagonal slash of dense ink-wash shadow cutting from upper-left to lower-right — two simultaneous moments in one frame, unified by darkness.

LEFT HALF: Hook's right forearm in extreme close-up, occupying the full left portion of the frame. The sleeve is deep crimson velvet, tarnished gold brocade catching diffuse lagoon light, the fabric worn at the wrist with the honest decay of a garment too fine for this life. His rapier is lowered — the blade angled downward and out of frame, defeated in this single small way. Across the back of his hand, a fresh cut from a dagger — narrow, precise, already welling. The blood is wrong. It is rendered in a deep, murky, brackish yellowish-brown — too dark, too thick, unmistakably not the expected crimson. It pools in the crease of his knuckle with an almost oily viscosity, and three drops fall toward the panel's lower edge, each one catching enough aqueous lagoon light to make their wrongness undeniable. The monochromatic ink-wash palette makes this aberrant colour the panel's single most vivid note. In the crosshatched background shadow, barely visible, a subtle scale-pattern in the hatching — Crocodile Shadow undertone, the texture of mortality.

RIGHT HALF: Hook's face in close-up, turned sharply toward his own wound, the angle of his gaze pulling his chin and jaw toward the left half of the frame. His Van Dyck beard is immaculate, his ringlet curls falling at the jaw like black candles. His blue eyes — that single warm, unexpected note in his dark palette — are wide. Not theatrically wide. Genuinely, privately wide. The magnificent rage is still there, carved into the set of his brow and the tension across his temples, but beneath it something has fractured by a single degree: the jaw is not quite set, the lower lid has lifted fractionally, the performance has cracked at its most vulnerable join. This is fear. Not the fear of pain or death or the dark water — a deeper, more shameful fear of what the wrongly-coloured blood means about what he is. The most human he will ever look. The most frightening he will ever look, for that exact reason. His hook — iron, catching whatever wan lagoon light filters through — is just visible at the frame's right edge, as though even now it reaches toward something it cannot name.

Light source: cold, filtered, aqueous — the diffuse turquoise half-light of the Mermaids' Lagoon at dusk, coming from below-left, casting upward shadows that make familiar features strange. No warm gold. Midnight Blue atmosphere throughout, Crocodile Shadow as the underpainting across both halves.

Edwardian ink-and-wash technique — Rackham-influenced dense crosshatching in the shadow diagonal, organic gestural linework on the coat fabric, precise controlled line on the face. No text, no lettering, no speech bubbles, no symbols.
