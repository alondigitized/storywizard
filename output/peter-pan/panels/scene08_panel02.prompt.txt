Style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are organic, gestural, and slightly irregular in Neverland (alive, dreamed, never quite stable), and precise, architectural, and controlled in London (orderly, safe, constrained). The overall aesthetic is a 'dark fairy tale': beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist in every image. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy; light sources are warm, golden, and magical. Crosshatching is used to build dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. The visual grammar shifts perceptibly between the two worlds: London panels have harder outlines and more formal compositions; Neverland panels breathe with softer borders and looser, more dynamic mark-making. The whole adaption should read as a storybook being remembered rather than one being told for the first time — every Neverland image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy — he is never quite still. His face is beautiful and slightly alien: large, luminous eyes (depicted without visible pupils in close-up, suggesting he perceives time differently from human children), a sharp chin, and a mouth perpetually on the edge of either a crow of triumph or a grin of pure mischief. His skin carries the permanent tan of a boy who has never been indoors in his life. He is drawn at a slight upward angle more often than not — the reader tends to see him from below, as the Darling children first see him, which preserves his mythic scale.; A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, and umber browns stitched together with what appears to be spider silk. The construction is irregular and organic, as though the forest itself assembled it during a particularly creative afternoon. A small red-feathered cap whose single feather is his one visible concession to vanity. Always barefoot. COMPOSITION: Medium interior shot from inside the nursery, camera angled toward the open window at three-quarter depth. The frame divides into foreground and background across a single threshold of light. Peter Pan occupies the sharp foreground right — perched in the open window frame, left bare foot hooked over the outer sill, body angled toward the night sky beyond, weight effortlessly distributed between inside and outside as though the choice of departure requires no more deliberation than breathing. His leaf-tunic runs deep russet rather than green, the sole concession to whatever he cannot name. His face turns back into the room — luminous pupil-less eyes wide with clean, unclouded curiosity, mouth in its edge-of-grin position, expression holding no sorrow, no longing, only the frank observational stillness of a creature watching an incomprehensible custom from an enormous distance. He casts no shadow. Tinker Bell hovers palm-sized at his right shoulder, precisely veined dragonfly wings catching cold air, her light shifted from her usual gold to a silver-white — the Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) of loyalty and self-transcendence — casting a hard cool counter-light across the window frame and falling in a clean bisecting line across Wendy's upturned face. Wendy stands mid-ground, her white nightgown bearing a grass-stain at the hem and a small tear at the left shoulder, body half-turned toward Peter — face split between two light sources simultaneously, the left side warmed by amber, the right side cooled to silver-blue, her periwinkle palette holding steady against both pulls, her expression carrying the bittersweet knowledge she has only now fully understood. In soft-focus background depth: Mrs. Darling kneels in her deep Midnight Blue dressing gown at the room's amber-lit center, hair loosely pinned, hands reaching toward a press of returned children, the unreachable-kiss highlight glowing at the right corner of her mouth, her softer line-weight making her radiate rather than resolve. Lost Boys cluster at the amber-lit periphery, half in inkwork shadow, watching with shy, hovering uncertainty. The open window frames a deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) night sky crowded with cold stars, London's gaslit amber rooftops visible far below — a different warmth, entirely unreachable. The panel's dominant light tension: Nursery Amber (#C8860A) pooling thick and golden from the room's left depth versus Tinker Bell's cold silver-white cutting from the right foreground window, two irreconcilable warmths meeting along a single diagonal line that runs through Wendy's face. Peter's silhouette is outlined against the night sky — his edges already beginning to dissolve into the dark he belongs to. Panel borders are straight and architectural throughout — London's visual grammar — except at the outer window edge where the line softens by the narrowest margin: the threshold rendered in the border itself.
