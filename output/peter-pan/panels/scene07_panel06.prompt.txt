He faces downward and slightly away from the viewer — not looking at us, not at any opponent, only at what waits beneath. Within the resignation: a last trace of dignity, almost serene. His remarkable blue eyes are cast downward. His shadow falls enormous and myth-scaled across the deck planking behind him, disproportionate to all physics.

Dominant colors: Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) for his coat, Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) saturating the surrounding sky and rigging, Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) rising from the water below as a visual undertone infecting the lower third of his silhouette. One faint gleam of a half-submerged eye catching no warmth. A slow, patient V-shaped wake spreading from a point slightly left of center, the wake of something enormous and unhurried. At the composition's focal center, spreading outward from a single disturbed point on the surface: perfect concentric ripples, rendered in fine pale ink lines against the dark — the water's memory of what just happened, still, calm, complete.

The entire panel breathes with silence and weight. No movement except the slowly spreading ripples. Somewhere just outside the image's boundary, implied rather than shown: the geometry of a clock face barely visible as faint hatching in the water's dark surface — a discovered detail, never labored.

Mood: elegiac, theatrical, inevitable. Beautiful and final.
