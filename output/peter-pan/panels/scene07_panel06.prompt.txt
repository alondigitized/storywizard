Near-splash panel composition bleeding on three sides, divided diagonally from upper-right to lower-left by a churning line of dark water. Edwardian ink-and-wash graphic novel art in the style of Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac — expressive, gestural pen linework with luminous watercolor washes, organic and slightly irregular mark-making, heavy crosshatched shadows, and the saturated visual grammar of a cherished memory.

**UPPER HALF — THE RAIL:** Captain Hook balanced on the Jolly Roger's wooden rail, one tall black boot planted on the wood, the other extended into open air above the void. His body is turned slightly sideways, coat of deep crimson velvet billowing dramatically outward, the tarnished gold brocade catching cold moonlight. His iron hook is raised high above his head — not in threat but in pure theatrical declaration, a final gesture made to no audience, to the night itself, to form. His jet-black ringlet curls lift in the sea wind. His face is angled slightly downward, looking at the dark water below — not at Peter, not at the reader. The long pale face beneath his bedraggled plumed hat carries an expression of complete, unguarded resignation — no fear, no defiance now, only a last, interior dignity. His remarkable deep blue eyes are visible, almost luminous against the pale face. His shadow on the ship's rail and deck is disproportionately enormous, stretching impossibly far — a mythic shadow belonging to legend, not to physics.

**THE DIAGONAL DIVIDE:** A churning dark seam of inky water rendered in loose, expressive brushwork strokes — the threshold between what was and what has already happened.

**LOWER HALF — THE WATER:** Deep, churning ocean surface rendered in rich ink-heavy brushwork with Midnight Blue and Crocodile Shadow washes — dark greens and deep blues swirling in inky, turbulent strokes.
