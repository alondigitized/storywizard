IMAGE PROMPT:
Tight horizontal strip panel on the Jolly Roger's main deck at deep night, strongly diagonal internal composition dividing the panel along an invisible axis of tension. Cold moonlight floods from directly above, splitting the deck into two distinct visual registers: Hook's right half drowning in heavy crosshatched ink shadow — the mainmast looming behind him like a dark pillar of doom, his enormous disproportionate shadow thrown wide across the deck planking regardless of light physics — while Peter's left half breathes in clean open linework and cold silver negative space, the moonlight finding him fully.

On the right: Hook backed hard against the mainmast, deep crimson velvet coat catching cold moonlight in its worn gold brocade, his iron claw raised at shoulder height but the arm visibly trembling. His long pale face with its precise Van Dyck beard remains magnificent — high-boned, aristocratic — but the blue eyes have cracked open: beneath the theatrical bravado lies something genuine and terrible, a man finally meeting something he cannot perform his way past. His black ringlet curls and plumed hat press against the mast. His body is contracted, angular, rigid — every line of him is withdrawal and resistance.

On the left: Peter advancing across the rain-slicked tilting deck, one bare foot higher than the other on the canted planking, body angled forward with perfect fluid balance. His leaf-tunic catches silver moonlight. He is smiling — not warmth, not mischief, but the bright uncanny expression of someone completely at home in the only moment that has ever mattered. A thin sword held in his scarred hand, the grip loose, almost careless — the grip of someone who does not need to grip. He moves left-to-right, visually refusing retreat.

Between them: a wide strip of moonlit grey-washed deck planking, the empty space enormous and charged. In the deep background, the silhouettes of Lost Boys form a loose encircling ring — torchlight warm amber-gold at the far edges of the composition, moonlight cold silver at its center.

Subtle crocodile scale-pattern worked into the crosshatching texture of the mainmast's shadow. No text, no lettering, no captions.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: small lithe boy of about twelve, leaf-and-forest-material tunic in russets and deep greens stitched with spider silk, red-feathered cap, always barefoot, luminous pupil-less eyes, shadow present but fractionally misaligned; dominant palette Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) with Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) in moonlit highlights. Captain Hook: tall cadaverous buccaneer, deep crimson velvet coat with tarnished gold brocade, lace cravat of impossible whiteness, iron hook replacing right hand, jet-black ringlet curls, blue eyes the single warm note; dominant palette Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) and Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B), shadow mythically oversized.

ART STYLE: Edwardian ink-and-wash, Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac linework, organic gestural mark-making, rich ink-heavy crosshatched shadows, Neverland night palette of Midnight Blue dominant with cold silver moonlight — slightly oversaturated, the too-vivid quality of a cherished and terrible memory. Neverland organic panel borders.
