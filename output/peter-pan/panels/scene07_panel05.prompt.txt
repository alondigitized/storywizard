IMAGE PROMPT:
Tight horizontal strip panel, strongly diagonal internal composition dividing the frame from lower-left to upper-right along an invisible axis of tension. The Jolly Roger's tilting deck at night — weathered dark planks slick with salt spray, the massive mainmast rising from the right edge of frame, its rigging cutting black geometry against a deep Midnight Blue sky flooded with cold silver moonlight. The moonlight is the scene's dominant light source: a high, pale wash striking from above-right, turning the deck between the two figures into a wide, luminous silver strip.

RIGHT SIDE — Hook backed hard against the mainmast, his great Hook's Crimson coat catching moonlight along its worn gold brocade, the tarnished velvet pooling in deep shadow. His iron claw is raised but the arm visibly trembles — the hook catching a blade of cold silver light. His body is contracted and rigid, shoulders drawn in, spine against the mast as if the wood itself is the last thing holding him upright. His face — high-boned, darkly handsome — is still magnificent, but the composition of bravado has cracked open: the profound blue eyes no longer performing, showing something genuine and terrible beneath, the horror of a man who has finally understood. Heavy crosshatched watercolor shadows saturate his half of the panel — Midnight Blue and Crimson pooling in the folds of the coat, his disproportionately large shadow sprawling behind him across the deck regardless of light physics, belonging to myth not physics.

LEFT SIDE — Peter advancing across the tilting deck, one bare foot planted higher on an angled plank than the other, body angled forward in perfect, fluid balance. He is completely open — no contraction, no guard — his arms and torso flowing as naturally as water finding its level. His thin sword is held loosely in his scratched hand, almost carelessly, the point low. He is smiling: not warmth, not kindness — the bright, uncanny smile of someone completely, terrifyingly at home in this exact moment. His half of the panel is rendered in clean watercolor lines and open space, cold silver-white light surrounding him, the deck clear and uncluttered, breathing room where Hook has none. His shadow is absent or barely present, not obeying the same light as everything else.

BACKGROUND — a loose ring of figures in torchlight and moonlight at the panel edges: small silhouettes of the Lost Boys, the orange-amber of torches marking their position and creating a warm counterpoint at the frame's periphery. Between the two central figures: wide empty moonlit deck, the heart of the panel — a void charged with everything that has been building since the beginning.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy. His face is beautiful and slightly alien: large, luminous eyes without visible pupils, a sharp chin, a mouth perpetually on the edge of mischief. Permanent tan. A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, umber browns stitched with spider silk. Small red-feathered cap. Always barefoot. Small dagger visible at his tunic. Scratched hands. Dominant colors: Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) and Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8).

ART STYLE — Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration with soft luminous washes and delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Characters with simple expressive faces and large emotive eyes, fluid poses. Deep indigo and silver night palette. Handpainted quality — alive, layered, nostalgic wonder carrying an undertow of genuine menace.
