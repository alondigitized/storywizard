Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga.

Low-angle medium two-shot from below and slightly to the left, looking up at the side of a dark wooden rowboat drawn against the flank of Marooners' Rock. The rock rises as a theater-stage monolith in the right background, its surface slick and black-grey under a night sky rendered in deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) with Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) pooling at the horizon. A single diagonal blade of cold moonlight cuts from upper-right to lower-left across the entire composition, striking the knife blade, catching the beadwork at Tiger Lily's wrists, and silvering the surface of the water where it is exposed — all else falls into shadow. The water directly beneath the boat's hull is rendered as flat, opaque black ink with no reflection and no movement, an absolute void. The visible water beyond the shadow shimmers with faint phosphorescent turquoise — a suggestion of the lagoon's unearthly depth — but the darkness at the hull swallows it. The organic Neverland panel border intrudes at the lower edge, a suggestion of submerged rock and weed.

Smee is half-risen from his rowing seat, broad body leaning toward the bow with the unhurried ease of a man completing a domestic task. His round, ruddy, apple-cheeked face carries its unvarying amiable pleasantness — he looks no more troubled than if tying a parcel. One hand holds a short-bladed working knife that catches the full slash of moonlight, the blade mid-sawing through a fraying rope at Tiger Lily's wrists. His free hand steadies himself on the gunwale. His striped knit cap sits squarely. A second oarsman in the stern is visible as a slumped silhouette, back to the viewer, one oar trailing — utterly indifferent.

Tiger Lily sits at the bow, bound wrists lifted toward Smee's knife but her body otherwise entirely self-contained. She does not look at Smee. Her gaze is directed straight ahead, out over the dark water, expression locked in cold, unreadable composure — neither grateful nor relieved, the expression of someone who considers rescue a matter of course but has decided to allow it. Even as the rope begins to fall in a loose curl into the bilge water, her spine is incrementally straightening, a millimeter of new dignity entering her posture. She draws herself taller without any visible muscular effort, as though pride is a physical substance refilling a vessel. The moonlight catches her structured feathered headdress and the beadwork detailing at her wrists — warm ochres and umbers against the pervasive blue-black of the night. A wash of Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) ghosts the near edge of the boat like a stain in the water, danger and courage indistinguishable in the same color.
