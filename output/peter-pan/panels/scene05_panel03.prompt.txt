Half-width vertical panel. Low angle, slightly elevated eye-line — as if the viewer crouches just off the forest path, watching the column pass. The frame is dominated by massive tree trunks rendered as ink-shadow columns, their bases vanishing into total darkness below, their canopies dissolving upward into deep purple-black. The forest is absolute night: Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) and deep violet, with only the faintest suggestion of depth in the layered shadow-work. Organic, irregular panel borders — a branch tip intrudes at the upper-left corner, reinforcing the Neverland visual grammar.

A war party moves through the mid-ground in near-silence. The warriors are rendered predominantly as silhouettes — dark shapes against slightly lighter dark, the only details a caught cheekbone here, the cold glint of a blade catching an unseen forward torch there. They compress into the center of the frame, moving left-to-right, bodies disciplined and close. No face in the column is fully visible except the last.

Tiger Lily occupies the rear third of the composition — closest to the viewer, closest to whatever follows behind. She is rendered with Pre-Raphaelite precision against the surrounding ink-darkness: every detail of her face, headdress, and ceremonial dress resolved with Arthur Rackham-quality linework while the world around her dissolves into shadow. A single narrow edge of torchlight from somewhere far ahead — warm ochre, barely reaching her — catches only the right side of her face: one high cheekbone, one dark eye, the trailing edge of a feather. Her spine is absolutely vertical. Her chin is level. The feathers of her ceremonial dress trail behind her like ink-brushstrokes dissolving into the darkness at the panel's edge, becoming part of the forest itself.

Her expression is neither defiance nor pride. It is simply the face of someone who has never considered any other way of moving through the world.

Crosshatched shadows build dimensional depth throughout — the tree trunks are built from dense parallel hatching that bleeds into the violet-black background wash. A faint suggestion of Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) lives in the darkest shadow-layers — the forest acknowledging its own. The overall tone is two registers darker than the adjacent panel, the palette stripped to deep purples, Midnight Blue, and the single warm sliver of torchlight.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Tiger Lily: Princess of the Piccaninni tribe — tall, with the absolute composed stillness of someone for whom movement is always a deliberate choice. High, strong cheekbones and a gaze of complete self-possession. Head always erect — her visual signature in every panel. Ceremonial dress in warm earth tones — ochres and deep umbers with decorative beadwork. Structured feathered headdress. Placed at the rear of the war-party column — the position of greatest danger and greatest honor. Color palette: Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914), Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) as undertone.

ART STYLE: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac. Dark fairy tale aesthetic. Shadows rich and ink-heavy. Crosshatching for dimensional shadow. Organic, slightly irregular Neverland panel borders. Oversaturated, too-vivid quality of cherished memory.
