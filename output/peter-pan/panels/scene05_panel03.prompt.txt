IMAGE PROMPT:
Half-width vertical panel. Camera placed low and slightly behind, at the level of moving feet — a ground-level perspective looking up the column of warriors into deep forest. The composition is a narrow corridor of near-darkness: ancient trees rise as massive ink-shadow columns flanking both sides of the frame, their canopy closing overhead so that only slivers of deep purple-midnight sky are visible between branches. The forest floor is layered shadow on shadow — Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) and bruised purple, with no ambient warmth. The war party fills the middle distance: twelve to fifteen figures rendered almost entirely as silhouettes in motion, shapes rather than faces — the barest glint of a blade-edge catching some unseen forward light, a glimpsed cheekbone, the white of an eye in shadow. They move with absolute silence encoded in their posture: weight forward, heads low, each figure occupying exactly the space they need and no more. They are the forest made briefly human.

At the very rear of the column, closest to the viewer, Tiger Lily emerges from the darkness — the single figure given full Pre-Raphaelite precision against the surrounding ink. A sliver of unseen torchlight from far ahead the column catches only the right side of her face: one high cheekbone illuminated in warm ochre-gold, the curve of a dark eye carrying cold fire, the strong line of her jaw. The rest of her falls into deep Midnight Blue shadow. Her spine is perfectly erect. Her head is perfectly erect. Her feathers trail behind her like ink-brushstrokes dissolving into the darkness. The decorative beadwork on her ceremonial dress catches the faintest warm glint along its edge — Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914) warm ochres against the surrounding cool dark. She does not scan the trees. She does not tighten. Her expression holds no pride, no defiance, no vigilance performed for an audience — only the absolute composure of someone who has never considered moving through the world any other way. She is the most still thing in the frame. She is the most powerful thing in the frame.

A single suggestion of Neverland Viridian — a moss-covered root, a trailing fern at panel edge — marks this as living Neverland even in near-total darkness. The panel border is organic, slightly irregular, the left edge interrupted by a dark branch-shadow pressing into the gutter.

No text. No lettering. No symbols.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Tiger Lily: tall, high strong cheekbones, gaze of complete self-possession, head always erect, spine straight regardless of context. Ceremonial dress in warm ochres and deep umbers with decorative beadwork, structured feathered headdress, feathers trailing. Placed at the column's rear — position of greatest danger and greatest honor. The most compositionally still figure in scenes of high movement. Color palette: Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914), Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E).

ART STYLE — Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration: soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Rich atmospheric depth. The world feels alive, layered, handpainted. Nostalgic wonder painted with the beauty and melancholy of a world you can never quite return to.
