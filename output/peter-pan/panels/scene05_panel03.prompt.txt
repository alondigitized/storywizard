Ancient trees rise as ink-shadow columns, their trunks so dense they absorb all surrounding light, their canopy swallowed entirely by the darkness overhead. A war party moves through this cathedral of shadow in near-perfect silence — a column of figures rendered almost entirely as silhouettes, their forms distinguishable only by the faintest recovered detail: the glint of a blade edge, the brief plane of a cheekbone catching some indeterminate light source from far ahead, the ghost of a trailing feather's edge. The column moves left-to-right through the frame, bodies compressed into shadow, occupying the left two-thirds of the panel in a procession that speaks of total discipline and absolute silence.

At the column's rear, separated by a full beat of darkness from the last silhouette ahead, Tiger Lily steps into a knife-edge of torchlight originating from somewhere far forward in the forest — its source unseen, its reach barely sufficient. This single thin ribbon of warm amber catches only the right side of her face and the forward plane of one shoulder. Against the enveloping darkness, this partial illumination renders her with the precise, luminous clarity of a Pre-Raphaelite oil study: the high architecture of her cheekbone, the dark eye carrying its cold fire, the utterly composed set of her mouth — an expression belonging to no named emotional category because it has never required one. Her head is perfectly, absolutely erect. Feathers from her ceremonial dress trail behind her like ink-brushstrokes dissolving into the surrounding dark. Scattered beadwork on her chest catches the torchlight as a single quiet constellation of warm points. The forest floor shows no disturbance behind her. The trees have closed without sound. The organic panel border at the right edge is interrupted by a single intruding shadow-branch from the darkness beyond — Neverland's irregular visual grammar confirming itself.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Tiger Lily: Princess of the Piccaninny tribe — tall, with the absolute composed stillness of someone for whom movement is always a deliberate choice. She has high cheekbones and dark eyes carrying cold fire. Ceremonial dress in warm earth tones — ochres and deep umbers with decorative beadwork that catches available light in precise points of warmth. Head always erect — this is her visual signature in every panel, whether alone or in crowds. In all war-party compositions, she is placed at the rear — the position of greatest danger and greatest honor. The most compositionally still figure in scenes of high movement — her stillness reads as power, not passivity.
