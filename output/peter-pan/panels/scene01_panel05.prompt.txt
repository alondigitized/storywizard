Style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are organic, gestural, and slightly irregular in Neverland (alive, dreamed, never quite stable), and precise, architectural, and controlled in London (orderly, safe, constrained). The overall aesthetic is a 'dark fairy tale': beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist in every image. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy; light sources are warm, golden, and magical. Crosshatching is used to build dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. The visual grammar shifts perceptibly between the two worlds: London panels have harder outlines and more formal compositions; Neverland panels breathe with softer borders and looser, more dynamic mark-making. The whole adaption should read as a storybook being remembered rather than one being told for the first time — every Neverland image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory.

LEFT ZONE — Mrs. Darling has lurched upright from her armchair, body twisted, one hand thrust outward palm-facing-forward, the other clutching the chair-arm. Her Midnight Blue dressing gown fans behind her with the violence of the movement. Loose-pinned hair disheveled. Her face is genuinely frightened — wide bright eyes, slightly open mouth — not theatrical alarm but the real expression of a mother confronting the inexplicable. Sewing and thread cascade in mid-air around her, the needlework caught at the apex of its scatter. The unreachable kiss highlight persists at the right corner of her mouth even in terror. Soft-focus line treatment; her figure marginally less defined than the harder world around her. Color: Midnight Blue dressing gown, Nursery Amber warmth emanating from her form, Wendy Periwinkle cool undertone at the hem.

The window frame fills the upper centre of the panel, its glass reflecting a sliver of moonlit London rooftops beyond — the threshold. Fairy Dust Silver trails faintly from his heels where he has just cleared the sill. He casts no shadow of his own.

RIGHT ZONE — Nana bursts through the nursery doorway with the full momentum of a Newfoundland's bulk, low and lunging, dark coat filling the doorframe. White nurse's apron streaming. Her jaws are open wide. The window sash has slammed down — depicted with hard architectural line, a guillotine of straight Edwardian precision. Nana's jaws are closed upon Peter's shadow. The shadow is rendered as a distinct, ink-black shape — a near-perfect silhouette of Peter himself, arms raised, head thrown back — filled with dense crosshatching that gives it mass and dimensional depth, unlike the kinetic gestural marks of every living figure. It is tearing free from where his feet last touched the sill, one arm of the shadow trailing across the nursery floorboards like a piece of cloth being pulled from a table. The shadow is the only static object in the panel.

Atmosphere: London-precise straight panel borders; aggressive kinetic energy held in a single frozen frame; three bodies in motion, one perfect arrested stillness — the shadow, bunched in Nana's jaws, real and dark and caught.
