Full-width nursery interior, wide cinematic frame capturing three simultaneous explosive actions held in precise horizontal triptych composition. The room is rendered in deep Nursery Amber candlelight — warm gold firelight from the left casting long controlled shadows across the nursery floor, architectural precision in every straight line and right angle.

LEFT THIRD: Mrs. Darling has lurched violently upright from her fireside chair — the chair itself tilting backward behind her. White sewing threads and scattered fabric arc through the air around her in a frozen spray. Her right arm is raised, palm outward, face turned toward the window with genuine wide-eyed terror — not theatre, but real animal alarm. Her deep warm-blue dressing gown is disheveled at the shoulder, hair half-pinned and falling loose. The unreachable-kiss highlight catches at the right corner of her open mouth. Her left hand still clutches a tangle of thread. She is rendered with slightly softer linework than the room around her, marginally less defined, more fragile.

CENTER: Peter Pan is fully airborne — body horizontal and dynamic, arms spread wide, bare feet leaving the sill. His expression is wild, grinning, entirely at home in midair even inside a small room. His leaf-tunic in deep viridian and russet, red-feathered cap, scraped bare feet. Below his feet, THE SHADOW — an ink-black shape distinct from him, almost a second boy, tearing free like cloth ripping, trailing one dark arm down toward the nursery floorboards. The shadow is rendered in dense, methodical crosshatching — the heaviest mark-making in the entire panel, stark against the amber floorboards. Peter himself carries no shadow — only this departing thing below him. He moves left-to-right, toward the window.

RIGHT THIRD: Nana fills the doorway she has burst through — enormous dark Newfoundland bulk, white nurse's apron twisted sideways, ears pinned back in full predatory charge. Her powerful shoulders low and driving forward, jaws wide open. The nursery window is caught mid-slam — depicted as a sharp descending line of cold moonlight being extinguished. Nana's jaws have closed on the trailing shadow-arm — the ink-black shape of the shadow bunched in her teeth, one shadow-arm stretching down to the floorboards. Peter himself is already gone — only glass and moonlight and the shadow remaining.

The nursery backdrop: three small beds in a row, toys on shelves, firelight amber on the walls, precise architectural linework throughout. The cold pale-silver rectangle of the closing window cuts against the warm interior amber — the first intrusion of night extinguishing itself. Gestural kinetic marks — arcing sewing threads, scattered cushion, Nana's momentum lines — everywhere except the crosshatched shadow, which is utterly still and final.
