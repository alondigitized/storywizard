What remains, clenched in Nana's jaw, is the shadow. The shadow is rendered in heavy crosshatching: dense, ink-black, a distinct figure — almost a second Peter — torn cleanly from his feet as cloth tears from a seam. One shadow-arm trails down onto the nursery floor, its crosshatched darkness pooling against the warm amber boards. Nana's expression holds both triumph and the intelligence of an animal who understands what has just occurred.
