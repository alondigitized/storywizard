TALL, NARROW VERTICAL PANEL. Peter Pan fills the entire frame, crouched low on a wooden window ledge, one scarred hand gripping the painted window frame, the other hovering free — weight distributed across bare feet with the effortless precision of a creature that treats surfaces as optional. He faces slightly inward, toward the room, his gaze mid-shift: the feral grin has acquired an edge of animal wariness — lips still curved but thinning, luminous pupil-less eyes fractionally narrowed, reading the interior the way a fox reads an open barn. His chin is still tilted at that cocky upward angle, but the whole body is coiled for retreat, muscle-memory overriding boldness. Wild dark hair catches moonlight. His leaf-tunic — russets, deep greens, umber browns, spider-silk stitched — blends with the night and then contradicts it. NO SHADOW falls from him on the ledge or frame.

Behind him, through the open window: London rooftops recede in careful Edwardian silhouette against a deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) sky torn open by one cold, full moon. Chimney smoke drifts in slow columns. A scattering of gaslit amber windows punctuate the rooftop distance — warm, small, unreachable. This is his world. That sky belongs to him. The room does not.

LIGHT: The nursery interior spills warm Nursery Amber (#C8860A) across the left edge of the panel — candlelight gold pressing from within, catching the side of his face and the curve of one shoulder, the only warmth that touches him. Against it, the cold silver-blue moonlight presses from behind. He exists exactly at the threshold: caught between two light sources, belonging to neither room. A ghost-thin thread of Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) runs through the shadows beneath his feet — his color, here in London, signaling intrusion.

The panel border is slightly softer than the architectural London gutters surrounding it — the straight-ruled edges fractionally loosened, the corners marginally rounded, as though his presence has already begun to dissolve the domestic visual grammar of the nursery sequence.
