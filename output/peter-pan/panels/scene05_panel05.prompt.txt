Near-splash, full-bleed panel with no border. The composition is formally symmetrical and frozen — as if time itself has been suspended mid-breath. Hook reclines in his ornate chariot at center-left, elevated and absolutely still, both cigars burning at the corners of his lips, their twin curls of smoke rising undisturbed into the torchlit darkness above. His forget-me-not blue eyes — the panel's single warm, human note — are fixed on some indeterminate middle distance, expression one of exquisite, theatrical boredom. His magnificent deep crimson velvet coat settles in perfect, untroubled folds. His right arm has moved — has already moved — the iron hook extended in one contemptuous, economical arc, now returning. The movement reads as completed: it happened in the instant before this frame.

At the bottom-right corner, Skylights is folding — a heavy, stumbling pirate in rough canvas and salt-stained wool, one broad hand outstretched toward the viewer, torchlight blazing full across his upturned face, his expression caught at the precise moment of understanding. His bulk is tipping sideways and down, boots losing the deck, body already beginning to disappear out of frame. He is the panel's motion. Hook is the panel's stillness.

At the far right edge, Smee stands in three-quarter profile — compact, round-cheeked, spectacled — watching with the mild, pleasant attention of a man observing a household chore reach its natural conclusion.

Deep in the background, a dark chorus of pirates: torchlit silhouettes in the gloom, mouths open, eyes wide, pressed back against rigging and rail — their collective reaction the only register of what has actually occurred.

Lighting is torchlight — fierce, directional, warm amber and deep coal shadow. The iron hook is the panel's brightest element: left nearly bare of watercolor wash, cold and clean, catching every available light while everything around it drowns in ink. The cigars' smoke catches the torchlight in fine silver threads. Ink is at its heaviest here — rich, absolute blacks pooling in every fold of the coat, every crease of shadow. Watercolor washes are deep crimson and coal grey throughout, with Hook's shadow falling impossibly large across the chariot's back regardless of light physics.

Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) dominant, Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) in the coat's shadows. Smee: compact, ruddy-faced, cheerful, round apple-cheeked face, bright eyes behind old-fashioned spectacles, striped knit cap, loose work shirt, amiable expression entirely unchanged from baseline. London Slate (#6B7280) and Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914).

Edwardian ink-and-wash, Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac linework. Organic, gestural, slightly irregular Neverland mark-making. Dark fairy tale aesthetic — beautiful and genuinely menacing. Crosshatching builds dimensional shadow. Oversaturated, too-vivid quality of a cherished, slightly terrible memory.
