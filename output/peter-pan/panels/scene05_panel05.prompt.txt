IMAGE PROMPT:
Near-splash panel, full bleed, no border. A wide, low-angle composition with formal bilateral symmetry — Hook's chariot centered in the frame, torchlight from flanking sconces carving the scene from absolute blackness. The moment is frozen in a single suspended instant of terrible economy.

CENTER-FRAME: Captain Hook reclines in his chariot with the absolute, unbroken stillness of a man whose repose has not been interrupted. His long pale face, precisely maintained black Van Dyck beard, and forget-me-not blue eyes are fixed on the middle distance — above and beyond the viewer — with an expression of exquisite, theatrical boredom. Both cigars continue to burn between his lips, twin threads of smoke curling undisturbed into the dark air above him. His magnificent deep crimson velvet coat settles in perfect, undisturbed folds. His shadow on the wall behind him is disproportionately vast — a mythic, looming shape that belongs to legend, not physics.

BOTTOM-LEFT CORNER: Skylights — a heavy, lumbering pirate in rough canvas and salt-stained wool — is folding toward the ground, his body already beginning its irreversible descent into the panel's lower frame. His right hand reaches upward in the torchlight, fingers splayed, face blazing with the cold illumination of understanding. His expression holds the single worst moment: not fear, but comprehension. His falling form is rendered in the heaviest ink and deepest coal shadow, folding into the panel's corner like something being set aside.

THE HOOK: Iron, cold, catching all available torchlight — depicted nearly bare of watercolor wash, clean and clinical against the surrounding deep crimson and shadow. It occupies the midpoint between Hook and Skylights: it has moved. That is all. One contemptuous, economical arc, and it has already begun its return. The cigars have not moved. The blue eyes have not moved.

RIGHT EDGE: Smee stands at the panel's margin, slightly cut by the frame, watching with the mild and entirely untroubled attention of a man observing a minor domestic task reach its natural conclusion. His round apple-cheeked face, old-fashioned spectacles, striped knit cap, and working clothes are rendered in muted London Slate and Lost Boy Earth — ordinary, domestic, vertiginous in context.

DEEP BACKGROUND: A dark chorus of pirates recedes into coal shadow and Midnight Blue — open mouths, wide eyes, torchlight catching the whites, rendered with rich absolute blacks and minimal wash. They understand something irreversible has happened.

Ink is at its heaviest in this panel. Watercolor washes in Hook's Crimson and coal shadow dominate. The hook itself is the panel's single cold, uncolored element — it catches all the light.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Smee: Hook's Irish bosun — compact, ruddy-faced, and cheerful in a way that should be reassuring and is instead quietly vertiginous. Round, apple-cheeked face of a country shopkeeper, small bright eyes behind old-fashioned spectacles. Standard pirate working clothes — striped knit cap, loose work shirt, heavy canvas trousers. Expression almost never varies — the same amiable pleasantness regardless of context. Color palette: London Slate (#6B7280), Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914).

ART STYLE: Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Characters with simple expressive faces and large emotive eyes. The world feels alive, layered, handpainted. Nostalgic wonder painted with beauty and melancholy.
