Style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are organic, gestural, and slightly irregular in Neverland (alive, dreamed, never quite stable), and precise, architectural, and controlled in London (orderly, safe, constrained). The overall aesthetic is a 'dark fairy tale': beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist in every image. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy; light sources are warm, golden, and magical. Crosshatching is used to build dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. The visual grammar shifts perceptibly between the two worlds: London panels have harder outlines and more formal compositions; Neverland panels breathe with softer borders and looser, more dynamic mark-making. The whole adaption should read as a storybook being remembered rather than one being told for the first time — every Neverland image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy — he is never quite still. His face is beautiful and slightly alien: large, luminous eyes (depicted without visible pupils in close-up, suggesting he perceives time differently from human children), a sharp chin, and a mouth perpetually on the edge of either a crow of triumph or a grin of pure mischief. His skin carries the permanent tan of a boy who has never been indoors in his life. He is drawn at a slight upward angle more often than not — the reader tends to see him from below, as the Darling children first see him, which preserves his mythic scale.; A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, and umber browns stitched together with what appears to be spider silk. The construction is irregular and organic, as though the forest itself assembled it during a particularly creative afternoon. A small red-feathered cap whose single feather is his one visible concession to vanity. Always barefoot. Her eyes are enormous and entirely without restraint. She is incandescent — she generates her own light, and this light colors every object and character nearby with gold, making her visible influence on scenes she is not the focus of. Every one of her poses is emphatic and total. She feels everything at maximum volume and her body communicates it all.; A dress cut from a single skeleton leaf — the veining of the leaf forming the gown's entire pattern and structure. It is cut low and square, slightly abbreviated, elegant in construction though minimal in material. The dress is always the warm olive-gold of a dried leaf, contrasting with the brighter, more living gold of the light she emits.; She emits light — her presence physically illuminates panels, casting warm golden shadows on nearby characters and objects, Her light changes color with emotional state: cool clear gold when content, hot amber-red when consumed by jealousy, silver-white when acting from love and courage, flickering grey-white when dying, Her dialogue in panels is rendered as musical notation clusters, twinkling star-arrangements, or bell-curve waveforms — never words, Her dragonfly wings are always precisely rendered — the veining structure is consistent and specific, not generic fairy wings, In distant or small panels she appears as only a moving point of warm light with a trailing sparkle-path, Her close-up inset panels are always circular or oval — a visual convention marking her scale and separateness from the main panel geometry; color palette: Tinker Bell Gold (#F5C518) — her primary color, the color of pure unmediated feeling, Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) — bleeds into her aura during moments of consuming jealousy, Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) — her color in moments of self-sacrifice and pure loyalty

SCENE — Full-width horizontal strip, aerial perspective slightly above and behind the children as they hang suspended over Neverland at night. Every trace of gold has been stripped from the world. The jungle mass is dense and illegible, its greens collapsed to Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) murk. In the bay, the Jolly Roger sits at anchor as a dense menacing silhouette, built entirely in dense layered crosshatching: tattered black flags hang limp from rigging drawn as scratched ink wounds across the indigo sky. A thin grey thread of smoke rises from the shore camp — barely visible, barely alive.

The children press into a tight huddle of warmth. Wendy, in her white nightgown — grass stain at hem, small tear at left shoulder, a faint new watermark appearing at the waist — has drawn her right arm around Michael, who presses his small stuffed bear against his chest, his round face no longer transparent with wonder, eyes wide and uncertain, cream-and-pale-blue striped pajamas luminous in Tinker Bell's glow. John grips his battered oversized Sunday top hat with both hands, spectacles slightly askew, leaning instinctively inward toward warmth. All three lean toward Peter.

Peter alone is still — positioned slightly forward of the group, leaf tunic shifted to deeper russets, the bright greens muted. He does not look back at the children. His gaze is directed downward at the island below, his expression subtly altered from every previous panel: not afraid, but knowing. His shadow is absent. He is the warmest presence in the frame and does not know it.

Her light is the only thing between the children and the Midnight Blue pressing in from every direction. The panel border is heavier and darker than any preceding panel — thick ink lines slightly uneven, bleeding at the corners as though the darkness is soaking into the page itself.
