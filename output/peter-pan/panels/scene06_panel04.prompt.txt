Tall, narrow vertical panel composition — a column of dread. Extreme low camera angle looking up from near the waterline, placing Captain Hook as a towering silhouette against the night sky of the Mermaids' Lagoon. The framing is formal and symmetrical, the composition reverent in the way one is reverent before something dangerous.

Captain Hook stands at the prow of a small rowboat, facing three-quarter right, his silhouette magnificent and wrong against the Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) sky. He is tall and cadaverous — angular, precise, occupying more visual space than physics permits. His face is long and pale, lit from below by cold moonlight: the precisely maintained jet-black Van Dyck beard, the ringlet curls of black hair dressed like black candles framing his jaw. His expression is not rage — it is the far more terrifying thing: cold, composed, intelligent fury. Jaw set. Those remarkable blue eyes — the single warm note in his dark palette — now burning with the controlled anger of a proud man who has been humiliated. Three-quarter view, the iron hook raised slightly, catching the moonlight like a scythe.

His coat is a 17th-century buccaneer lord's crimson velvet (Hook's Crimson #8B0000) shot through with tarnished gold brocade — Edmund Dulac precision in every braid and button, the gold trim losing threads, the velvet worn at the elbows. The lace cravat impossibly white. The plumed hat silhouetted magnificently against the sky, its bedraggled feathers precise in detail. Two cigars rest in their custom holder, their smoke curling upward into the panel's organic border. The double cigar holder is clearly visible — his signature.

Behind him in the lower third of the panel, the pirate at the oars has gone completely still — a hunched, terrified shape rendered in shadow, deliberately small against Hook's dominance. Their stillness rhymes with the water.

The lagoon water around the rowboat is perfectly, unnervingly still — a dark mirror in Midnight Blue and silver, the moon's reflection undisturbed. In the far background, barely resolved in mist, the outline of Marooners' Rock. The surface disturbed nowhere.

Hook's shadow falls disproportionately large across the water behind him — far larger than physics or light source would permit, belonging to myth rather than moonlight. It spills down into the foreground like a declaration.

The linework shifts deliberately: Hook's coat and hat are rendered with Dulac's architectural precision — controlled crosshatching, every braid exact — while the water and mist behind him breathe with looser, organic Rackham mark-making. Rich ink-heavy shadows build dimensionally in the coat's folds. The panel border is slightly more formal and controlled than surrounding Neverland panels, encoding his theatricality in the composition itself.

Palette: Midnight Blue dominant, Hook's Crimson burning at the coat, cold silver moonlight catching the iron hook and the water's surface. A subtle Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) underpainting in the mist. No warmth anywhere. The mood is dread made beautiful.
