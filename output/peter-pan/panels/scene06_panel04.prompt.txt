Tall, narrow vertical panel composition — a column of dark authority. Captain Hook stands upright in the prow of a wooden rowboat at the lagoon's edge, rendered from a low, slightly reverential angle that makes him tower against the night sky. His silhouette is magnificent and wrong: the great plumed hat with its bedraggled feathers, the deep crimson velvet coat with tarnished gold brocade catching the moonlight, the impossible white lace cravat a ghost of brightness against the dark. His body is held with the rigid, studied composure of a man performing mastery over his own fury — not pantomime rage but something colder and more architectural.

His face in three-quarter view: long, pale, cadaverous, the precisely maintained black Van Dyck beard framing a jaw set like stone. His remarkable, profound blue eyes burn with cold, intelligent fury — the eyes of a man of education and sensitivity who has been made a fool of, and knows it, and will not forgive it. The double cigar holder barely perceptible at his lips, a thread of smoke curling upward into the mist. His iron hook is raised slightly at his side, catching the moonlight like a scythe, its curved edge the most light-struck element in the frame.

Immediately behind him, the rowing pirate has frozen at the oars — hunched, diminished, afraid to move or breathe, his eyes averted from his captain. He occupies the lower third of the panel, his small terrified form emphasizing by contrast how completely Hook fills the upper two-thirds.

The lagoon water around the boat is perfectly, unnervingly still — a black mirror reflecting the Midnight Blue sky and Hook's disproportionately vast shadow, which sprawls across the water's surface at an impossible angle, belonging to myth rather than physics. In the stillness, the faint bioluminescent glow of the lagoon's depths pulses beneath the surface — cool silver-aqua light from below, too beautiful, too wrong.

The sky above is deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) with scattered cold stars. No warmth anywhere except the dull ember of the cigar. The mist at the lagoon's edges is rendered with pale silver watercolor wash.

Dulac-inspired precision governs every detail of Hook's coat and hat — each braid, each gold thread, each button rendered with meticulous ink linework — while the water and mist around him breathe with the looser, organic Rackham-influenced mark-making of a Neverland night. Crosshatching builds deep dimensional shadow across his figure. His shadow on the water is ink-heavy and enormous.

Color palette: Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) dominant in the coat, Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) in sky and water, Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) as underpainting wash across the entire scene, cold silver moonlight as the sole secondary light source.
