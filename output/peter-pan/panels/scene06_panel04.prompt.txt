Tall narrow vertical panel, low-angle shot looking up at Captain Hook standing at the prow of a small wooden rowboat at the edge of the Mermaids' Lagoon. The low reverential camera angle makes him loom against the night sky, his silhouette dominating the upper two-thirds of the composition — the great plumed hat with its slightly bedraggled feathers, the magnificent long crimson velvet coat with tarnished gold brocade catching cold moonlight, the iron hook raised slightly at his side catching silver light like a scythe-blade. His face is in three-quarter view, jaw set, long pale face framed by jet-black ringlet curls dressed like black candles. His eyes — rendered with unusual care, the single warm Midnight Blue note in his dark palette — burn with cold, contained fury, not theatrical rage but the terrifying stillness of an intelligent man's genuine wrath. Van Dyck beard precisely maintained. The double cigar holder is clenched in his teeth, twin threads of smoke curling upward into the mist. His shadow falls massive and disproportionate behind him across the water's surface, belonging to myth rather than to light physics.

Behind Hook, slightly lower in the frame, the pirate oarsman sits completely frozen at the oars, knuckles white, eyes wide, a man made small and still by proximity to genuine danger. His ordinary pirate working clothes contrast with Hook's magnificence.

The lagoon water surrounding the boat is perfectly, unnervingly still — not the stillness of calm but the held-breath stillness of something wrong. The unearthly turquoise of the lagoon is muted in this deep night register, the surface a mirror of Midnight Blue and silver. Wisps of pale mist drift low across the water at the boat's hull. In the far distance, the suggestion of the mermaid's rock — now empty.

Dominant palette: deep Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) sky and water, Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) coat rendered with Dulac-inspired precision — every braid, every tarnished gold button, every worn elbow of velvet meticulously detailed — against the organic looseness of mist and water. Silver-white moonlight rakes across the hook, the white lace cravat of impossible cleanliness, the still water's surface. His shadow carries an underpainting of Crocodile Shadow grey-green.

Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor technique: soft luminous washes for the night sky and mist, precise hand-drawn linework for Hook's coat and hat — Dulac-level decorative detail on the man himself against painterly, breathing atmosphere around him. Panel borders are organic and slightly irregular, characteristic of Neverland night sequences. Hook's panel composition is formally symmetrical, the boat centered in the narrow vertical frame, his figure the apex. The feeling: cold magnificence, intelligence turned to fury, the most dangerous silence in Neverland.
