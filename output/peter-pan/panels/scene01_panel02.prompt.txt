Wide-angle shot of the nursery window dominating the panel, viewed from inside the room in the dark middle distance — the window fills the upper two-thirds of the frame. The room is in deep amber shadow, the candlelight extinguished or forgotten. Through a hairline crack in the window latch, a single irrational point of light has pierced the glass: Tinker Bell Gold (#F5C518), incandescent and needling, trailing a hard comet-arc of white-gold across the centre of the composition like a star being thrown sideways. The light ricochets off an oval looking-glass above the mantelpiece in the background, fracturing into two or three hard angular reflections that slash across the upper corners of the panel. The ordinary nursery objects — a wooden rocking horse mid-left, a folded cream towel draped over the brass fender below the mantelpiece, and two small framed watercolours on the right wall — are all caught in this impossible illumination, their shadows thrown backwards and upward in directions no candle or lamp could produce. The shadows are ink-heavy, cross-hatched, deeply dimensional, falling the wrong way against the warm Nursery Amber walls. At the absolute centre of the light-burst, barely discernible within the glare, is Tinker Bell: palm-sized, her arms flung wide in a pose of total imperious delight, dragonfly wings spread and precisely veined, the olive-gold skeleton-leaf dress visible only to a careful eye. She is more light than figure — a luminous smear of gold with the suggestion of form at its heart. The light bloom violates the panel border: a soft bleed of Tinker Bell Gold bleeds past the ruled edge on all four sides, the straight London border physically unable to contain it — the first intrusion of Neverland's visual grammar into the architectural order of the nursery. The room behind is all controlled crosshatching and precise Edwardian line, warm Nursery Amber deepening to Midnight Blue in the corners, while the window itself becomes a rupture — the impossible gold eating the domesticity outward from its centre. No figures in the room. Only the light, the shadows thrown the wrong way, and the tiny gleeful presence no one has seen yet.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Tinker Bell: No larger than a human hand — she is drawn to scale in wide establishing shots. She is incandescent, generating her own light. In distant or small panels she appears as only a moving point of warm light with a trailing sparkle-path. Her dragonfly wings are always precisely rendered — veining structure consistent and specific. Dress cut from a single skeleton leaf, warm olive-gold, cut low and square. Her light is cool clear gold when content. Arms wide, expression imperious and gleeful — emphatic and total.

ART STYLE — Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes. Detailed expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac. London panels: precise, architectural, controlled, straight borders, formal compositions. Shadows rich and ink-heavy. Crosshatching builds dimensional shadow. Dark fairy tale aesthetic — beautiful and slightly unsettling.
