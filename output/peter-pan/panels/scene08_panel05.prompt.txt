IMAGE PROMPT:
Wide three-quarter-page interior, night. The Darling nursery — but grown. Precise architectural London linework, straight panel borders, controlled crosshatching. The window is blown open dead center, curtains billowing inward on both sides, the same cold stars visible in the Midnight Blue sky beyond. But the room has quietly shifted: proper bookshelves, a woman's brush and comb on the dresser, adult furnishings with ordered Edwardian solidity. Everything has grown up except one small child's bed in the far corner.

The composition forms a triangle of gazes that never fully completes. At the window — framed and centered — Peter Pan crouches at the sill with his characteristic easy sovereignty, leaning inward, face turned toward Jane. Tinker Bell's cold incandescent gold floods from directly behind him, her light merciless and beautiful, throwing his silhouette forward into the room and washing everything in Tinker Bell Gold (#F5C518). He casts no shadow — or his shadow falls at an impossible angle, wrong and independent.

In the corner bed, full in Tink's cold gold light: Jane, seven years old, sitting bolt upright, one thin arm stretched unconsciously upward, her enormous dark eyes open and luminous, her face pure wonder — an echo the reader has seen before. She is reaching toward something she doesn't yet have a name for.

In the far left doorway, nearly consumed by deep ink shadow: adult Wendy in a pale dressing gown, Wendy Periwinkle (#7B9DC4) cooling to London Slate (#6B7280) at its shadowed edges. She is absolutely still. Her face is turned toward Peter. He has not turned toward her. Her expression is the still surface of deep water — love, loss, recognition, and something that has made its peace with itself, all held perfectly quiet. No urgency. No movement. A woman watching a boy who will never see her.

Lighting: Tinker Bell's cold gold dominates from the window, bleaching the curtains and illuminating Jane in full. It does not reach the doorway. Wendy stands in deep shadow, the old Nursery Amber (#C8860A) present only as a ghost undertone behind her — warmth that belonged to another version of this room. A subtle Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) underlies the floorboards between Wendy and the window, a barely-visible underpaint of time.

The image breathes. No urgency. Elegiac. Beauty that has already become memory.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy — he is never quite still. His face is beautiful and slightly alien: large, luminous eyes (depicted without visible pupils in close-up, suggesting he perceives time differently from human children), a sharp chin, and a mouth perpetually on the edge of either a crow of triumph or a grin of pure mischief. His skin carries the permanent tan of a boy who has never been indoors in his life. He is drawn at a slight upward angle more often than not — the reader tends to see him from below, as the Darling children first see him, which preserves his mythic scale. A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, and umber browns stitched together with what appears to be spider silk. The construction is irregular and organic, as though the forest itself assembled it during a particularly creative afternoon. A small red-feathered cap whose single feather is his one visible concession to vanity. Always barefoot. No shadow, or shadow misaligned and independent. Color palette: Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E), Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8), Nursery Amber (#C8860A) bleeding in only near Wendy. | Tinker Bell: No larger than a human hand — present here as pure radiant light source flooding through the window behind Peter, her incandescent Tinker Bell Gold (#F5C518) cold and merciless and beautiful, casting golden-edged shadows across curtains, bed, and child. Her emotional state here is complex — cool clear gold rather than jealous amber, the light of someone who has seen this before. Dragonfly wings precisely rendered with consistent veining structure. Olive-gold skeleton-leaf dress.

Art style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are precise, architectural, and controlled in London — orderly, safe, constrained. The overall aesthetic is a dark fairy tale: beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy. Crosshatching builds dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. Straight, precise panel borders. The image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory — a storybook being remembered rather than told for the first time.
