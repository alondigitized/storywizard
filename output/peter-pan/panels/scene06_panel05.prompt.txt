Style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are organic, gestural, and slightly irregular in Neverland (alive, dreamed, never quite stable), and precise, architectural, and controlled in London (orderly, safe, constrained). The overall aesthetic is a 'dark fairy tale': beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist in every image. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy; light sources are warm, golden, and magical. Crosshatching is used to build dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. The visual grammar shifts perceptibly between the two worlds: London panels have harder outlines and more formal compositions; Neverland panels breathe with softer borders and looser, more dynamic mark-making. The whole adaption should read as a storybook being remembered rather than one being told for the first time — every Neverland image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory.
CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Peter Pan: A boy of about twelve, small and lithe with a body that seems assembled entirely from stored kinetic energy — he is never quite still. His face is beautiful and slightly alien: large, luminous eyes (depicted without visible pupils in close-up, suggesting he perceives time differently from human children), a sharp chin, and a mouth perpetually on the edge of either a crow of triumph or a grin of pure mischief. His skin carries the permanent tan of a boy who has never been indoors in his life. He is drawn at a slight upward angle more often than not — the reader tends to see him from below, as the Darling children first see him, which preserves his mythic scale.; A tunic assembled from overlapping autumn leaves and forest materials — russets, deep greens, and umber browns stitched together with what appears to be spider silk. The construction is irregular and organic, as though the forest itself assembled it during a particularly creative afternoon. A small red-feathered cap whose single feather is his one visible concession to vanity. Always barefoot. His clothing subtly shifts with Neverland's emotional register: brighter greens in triumph, deeper russets in the rare moments he brushes against feeling.; No shadow in early scenes — his shadow is absent or behaves as an independent entity, establishing one of the story's first uncanny signals, Luminous eyes without visible pupils in close-up panels — beautiful but inhuman, the visual shorthand for his apartness from ordinary childhood, The crow gesture: arms wide, head thrown back, a position of absolute unselfconscious joy — his signature pose used for chapter-opening panels, A small dagger tucked into his leaf-tunic, always visible but rarely foregrounded — a reminder that Neverland's play is real, His hands carry small permanent scratches from tree-climbing and fighting — the only marks that accumulate on him across the story, He is never depicted in profile facing right — always moving left-to-right or toward the viewer, visually refusing retreat, In panels where other characters are aging or changing, Peter remains visually static — same posture, same brightness, same scale; color palette: Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) — his dominant color, the living green of permanent wildness, Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) — surrounds him during flight and moments of triumph, Nursery Amber (#C8860A) — bleeds into his panels only when he is physically close to Wendy, her warmth touching him without his awareness

CAPTAIN HOOK (previously established, maintain exact appearance): Tall, cadaverous, magnificently sinister — long pale face, precisely maintained jet-black Van Dyck beard, ringlet curls dressed like black candles. Deep blue eyes — the single warm note in his dark palette. Deep crimson velvet buccaneer coat shot through with tarnished gold brocade, fraying at elbows, paired with miraculously white lace cravat, tall black boots, bedraggled plumed hat. Iron hook replacing right hand, always catching available light. Shadow disproportionately large regardless of physics. Double cigar holder nearly always present. Color palette: Hook's Crimson (#8B0000) dominant, Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) for oceanic melancholy, Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) as underpainting.

COMPOSITION: Near-full-width panel, slight low angle. Two figures centered on a slick, wave-worn rock rising from absolute blackness — the lagoon consumes every horizon. The moon is enormous and blazing directly behind both figures, reducing them to dense, architectural silhouettes rimmed in cold silver — their forms caught in that single suspended beat before violence. Peter is left of center, crouched low and coiled, dagger arm fully extended toward Hook, bare feet splayed on wet stone, body a diagonal slash of kinetic energy moving left-to-right. Hook presses from the right, rapier leveled, iron hook raised high, great crimson coat billowing outward — his shadow spreads enormous across the rock face behind him, defying all physics. Between them: nothing but moonlit air and the weight of the moment.

ENVIRONMENT AND LIGHT: The moon — silver-white, occupying the upper third of the panel — is the sole light source. Its reflection fractures violently in the black lagoon water surrounding the rock, shattering into jagged silver shards. The water runs past the organic, irregular Neverland panel border and bleeds into the gutter. Rackham crosshatching builds under both figures into dense, almost architectural shadow volumes. The rock's wet surface catches moonlight in thin, precise highlights. The lagoon is Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) and absolute — no horizon, no shore, nothing beyond. The silhouetted figures carry just enough internal detail to read their forms and tension: the gleam of the hook's iron, the silver edge of Peter's dagger, the billow of the crimson coat reduced to dark mass.

MOOD: Sublime and dangerous — two myths recognising each other across three feet of wet rock, the world reduced to this stone, this steel, this moon. Wonder and genuine mortal menace sharing one breath. Exhilarating and elegiac simultaneously.
