A coal fire burns low in the grate at left, casting soft, directional orange light across the wooden floorboards in long warm bands. Heavy crosshatching builds depth across the walls and ceiling — ink layered with disciplined, architectural control — while the firelight areas dissolve into softer, looser watercolor washes.

Her body has relaxed into genuine sleep — head tilted slightly to one shoulder, chin dipped, the careful architecture of her waking posture softened entirely. A half-finished piece of white sewing has collapsed across her lap and over the chair arm. Her face is peaceful, unguarded, beautiful — the mocking quality gone from her mouth, leaving only warmth. Her deep warm-blue dressing gown pools around the chair legs. Hair loosely pinned at the nape. One hand rests open, palm upward, still oriented faintly toward the beds behind her.

In the mid-ground, three small beds are arranged in a precise row against the far wall, their sleeping occupants barely visible — small shapes under neat coverlets, breathing quietly in the amber dark. Each bed is orderly, symmetrical, safe.

Near the nursery door at right, Nana's empty kennel sits with its entrance facing the room — its emptiness a deliberate void in the composition, the one asymmetrical note in the room's protective geometry. A white nurse's apron is folded over the kennel's edge.

No green appears anywhere in this panel. The palette is entirely warm amber, deep shadow-brown, and the faint cool Midnight Blue of Mrs. Darling's dressing gown — the only intrusion of longing into a room otherwise sealed in gold.
