IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme close-up portrait: a large Newfoundland dog's face fills the entire panel frame edge to edge, centred and monumental, the muzzle occupying the lower third of the composition, the dark intelligent eyes positioned just above centre line. The camera is level — no angle, no tilt — giving the image the gravity of a formal portrait, a face confronting the viewer without evasion. The background is absolute darkness, featureless black ink, so the face appears suspended in void with no environmental context, no depth cues, nothing to diminish its singular weight.

The expression is not grief performed but grief endured: the deep-set eyes are entirely steady, pupils catching a single pinpoint of warm amber light, the brow-ridges carrying patient sorrow rather than anger — the face of intelligence that has witnessed something irreversible and chosen not to protest it. The ears lie gently back, not flattened in fear but lowered in quiet resignation. The enormous jaw and muzzle are still.

A single tear rolls slowly down the dark fur of the left cheek — oversized, deliberately so, occupying visual prominence no natural tear earns. It is tinted deep arterial red, a rich garnet crimson, rendered in transparent Rackham-style watercolour wash over the ink linework so the dark fur shows through it like a stained glass window. The tear catches the single light source — warm amber, directional from slightly above — and refracts it, glowing from within like a polished semi-precious stone. This is the panel's focal point: everything else organises itself around this one drop of red.

Dense crosshatching builds dimensional shadow around the eyes and along the muzzle's ridgeline — layered hatching that gives the face architectural depth without relying on flat fills. The fur is rendered in fine, directional ink strokes following the grain of the coat, each stroke purposeful, building texture that gives weight and substance to the darkness of the coat. The warm amber pinpoint light rakes across the brow ridge and catches the top of the muzzle, creating a single restrained highlight that makes the surrounding darkness more absolute.

The white nurse's apron is barely visible at the extreme lower edge of the frame — a fragment of white, almost cropped away entirely, its presence a half-seen detail rather than a compositional element. The face dominates everything.
