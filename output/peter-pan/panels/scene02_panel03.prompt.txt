IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme close-up, perfectly frontal and fractionally below eye level — Nana's face fills the entire frame from edge to edge, suspended in absolute darkness. No background, no room, no world: only her face and the void pressing close around it. Her muzzle occupies the lower two-thirds of the panel; her eyes dominate the upper half, descending toward the viewer with quiet, unbreakable gravity.

A single oversized tear rolls down the dark fur of her left muzzle — impossibly large, rendered in a deep Rackham-style watercolour wash of arterial crimson-red, translucent and luminous, catching the ambient light from within like a faceted garnet. This tear is the panel's gravitational centre. Every compositional element orbits it. Its redness is not blood but something older: grief distilled to a jewel, sorrow made permanent and visible.

Dense crosshatching in ink over watercolour builds dimensional shadow around her orbital hollows, giving her brow ridges and eye sockets architectural weight and depth. The hatched lines darken toward the corners and recede toward the bridge of her muzzle, making the tear catch and hold its faint warm light all the more sharply against the surrounding shadow. The only ambient illumination is a barely perceptible residue of warm candleglow from below-left — just enough to define the topography of dark fur, the contour of her muzzle, the still surface of her eyes — before the darkness reclaims everything.

Her eyes are steady and fully open: not angry, not accusing, not collapsed in grief. They hold the viewer without flinching, without self-pity, with the patient intelligence of a creature who has understood something the humans around her have not yet grasped, and who has chosen, quietly, to spare them the sight of what her understanding costs her. This is bottomless sorrow as dignity. The still point around which the scene pivots.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Nana: A large, distinguished Newfoundland dog — substantial and dark-coated, with the patient deep-set eyes of an animal who understands considerably more than she is given credit for. Her expressions communicate complex maternal intelligence without anthropomorphic exaggeration: raised brow-ridges, direct sustained gaze, the precise angle of her ears. Eyes of unusual intelligence — more emotionally readable than most human characters in the book. A white nurse's apron faintly visible at the base of frame before dissolving into darkness. Color palette: Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914) — grounded, warm, absolutely dependable; Nursery Amber (#C8860A) — reduced here to the faintest residual glow against the enclosing dark, warmth almost extinguished.

Art style: Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration with soft luminous washes and delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga, crosshatched ink shadow construction in the Arthur Rackham tradition. Large emotive eyes, expressive faces, the overall feeling of nostalgic wonder painted with the beauty and melancholy of a world you can never quite return to.
