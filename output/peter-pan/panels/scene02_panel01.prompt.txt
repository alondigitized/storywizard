Wide shot, Edwardian master bedroom at evening, slightly elevated camera angle looking across the room toward a tall mahogany pier-glass mirror. The room is geometrically immaculate — wallpaper in precise repeating diamond pattern, every cufflink box squared on the dresser, a row of polished shoes aligned beneath the wardrobe — order enforced down to the millimeter. The single point of chaos is Mr. Darling, positioned center-frame before the mirror, close enough that his reflection fills nearly half the composition: two versions of the same humiliation, doubled.

His face is a deep mottled scarlet from brow to jaw, coloring uneven in the way of suppressed rage rather than exertion. His jaw is clenched, his lips pressed into a flat line of wounded dignity. At his right temple a vein stands visible. He stares at his own reflection with the expression of a man who has been publicly defeated by an inanimate object and cannot find anywhere to direct his shame.

The light gilds the mirror's frame in Nursery Amber (#C8860A) but Mr. Darling himself sits in London Slate shadow, his own body resisting the warmth of the room. The mirror catches the gaslight directly and reflects it back as a secondary source, creating a subtle trap of doubled illumination around his humiliated figure.

His presence is the panel's quiet emotional puncture.
