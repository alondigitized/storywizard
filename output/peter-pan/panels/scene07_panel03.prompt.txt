Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Radial burst composition: the panel itself is shattering outward from the center, two sides entirely borderless — warm gold light has dissolved the frame and spills beyond the page edge into pure white. Tinker Bell burns at the absolute center of the image, suspended mid-air, no longer a guttering spark but a small incandescent sun. She is blazing — her light rendered in oversaturated Tinker Bell Gold (#F5C518), so intense it bleaches the tones of everything adjacent: nearby shadow edges dissolve into pale gilt, the surrounding darkness retreats. Concentric rings of warm light expand outward from her in soft watercolor halos — each ring a slightly different temperature, cycling through amber, warm gilt, and pale rose, like ripples across a golden pond, rendered in broad luminous washes with no crosshatching whatsoever. The glow is total, airborne, trembling. Her expression is imperious and fierce and wholly alive — the full, infuriating, magnificent force of her personality restored, her enormous eyes blazing with feeling at maximum volume. Her dragonfly wings are fully extended, their precise veining catching and refracting the radial light. In the lower-left corner, pressed against the soft remains of the panel border, Peter's face emerges from warm shadow — the grin has returned, wide and certain, but his eyes carry something new and unguarded: pure relief, real and unmasked, a fraction of a second before the performance of boyhood could reassemble itself. His luminous pupil-less eyes are soft in this instant, open in a way they almost never are. The surrounding atmosphere is entirely warm — Nursery Amber (#C8860A) and Tinker Bell Gold flooding every edge, driving back Midnight Blue to the extreme outer margins where the panel has not yet dissolved. No cold tones survive within the radial burst. The broken panel borders on two sides are rendered as ragged tears of light, not darkness — the gold itself has eaten through the frame. Light is the entire compositional grammar of this panel. No text, no words, no lettering, no speech bubbles.
