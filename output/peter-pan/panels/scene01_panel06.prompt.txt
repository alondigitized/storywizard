IMAGE PROMPT:
Ground-level camera angle, positioned just above the nursery floorboards, frame held low and wide — the viewer's eye at the height of a sleeping child. The composition is formal and horizontal, ruled borders precise and architectural, every line straight and controlled. The dominant subject is a crumpled shadow lying on the warm amber floorboards: flat, ink-black, with no body to cast it. It lies like a piece of discarded dark cloth — one shapeless arm-form reaching toward the window frame, one reaching back toward the room's interior, arrested mid-decision between two worlds. The shadow has no owner. It is simply there.

The floorboards glow with deep Nursery Amber (#C8860A) — warm wood grain rendered in careful layered washes, the amber shifting where cold light interrupts it. A thin strip of glacial blue-white moonlight cuts beneath the window at the far edge of the frame, cold Fairy Dust Silver (#C8D8E8) pressed against the glass — the only cool tone in an otherwise warm room. The contrast between amber floor and cold moonlight strip is the panel's central tension: two temperatures, two worlds, meeting exactly where the shadow lies.

Just left of the shadow: Mrs. Darling's dropped sewing — a small crumpled mound of cream fabric, a needle catching the firelight, a thread trailing loose across the floorboards toward the shadow's edge. Abandoned mid-stitch. Evidence of interrupted order.

Deep in the background, barely resolved against the amber warmth: the nursery fireplace, grate glowing with the last orange-red coals of a coal fire, breathing warmth into the upper third of the composition. Between the viewer and that glow: the shadow, flat and wrong, absorbing light rather than participating in it.

The room is entirely still. No movement. The only suggested motion is in the shadow's frozen, indecisive reach — one direction and then the other, neither completed. The formal composition presses in around it: straight-edged panel borders, an orderly room's careful geometry. The shadow does not belong to that geometry. It is the first wrong note in a safe room.

Subdued, intimate watercolor treatment — floor in warm amber washes with visible grain, fire in soft ember-orange, moonlight in a single cold blue-white blade. The shadow rendered in flat India-ink black with no gradient or softening. Panel borders straight and precise — London's architectural visual grammar in full authority. The scale is intimate, the palette muted, the silence complete.

No characters are present in the frame.

CHARACTERS REFERENCED BY ABSENCE — Mrs. Darling's sewing (cream fabric, needle, trailing thread) establishes her recent presence: a lovely, gentle woman whose world is organized and tidy. The abandonment of the sewing reads as the first disruption in a room that is otherwise entirely her warmth made visible — Nursery Amber (#C8860A) as her emotional emanation, now surrounding something she does not yet know to fear.

ART STYLE: Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous washes, delicate hand-drawn linework, meticulous domestic detail, the overall feeling nostalgic wonder edged with melancholy. Every background a landscape worth lingering in.
