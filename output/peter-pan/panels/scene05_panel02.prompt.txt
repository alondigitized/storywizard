IMAGE PROMPT:
Ground-level, tight low-angle shot pushing through dense Neverland jungle ferns at night — the camera is almost floor-level, looking up through a tunnel of enormous luminous leaves. TOOTLES emerges from the dark undergrowth directly toward the viewer, filling the center-lower frame, bear-skin hood drawn close around his face, dagger gripped in both hands held before his chest in a posture halfway between warrior and supplicant. His expression is one of concentrated, earnest hope — eyes large and slightly upward-tilted, mouth set with quiet determination, the face of someone who has placed himself in the path of the adventure one more time and genuinely believes this time it will notice him.

A single narrow shaft of moonlight falls precisely on his face and hands — nowhere else. The illumination is too clean, too specific, the oversaturated vivid quality of a memory replayed until it glows. His dagger catches a sliver of silver light. The ferns on either side are enormous, rendered in luminous Neverland Viridian watercolor washes layered over deep ink, their translucent greens backlit by some indeterminate jungle glow. Behind Tootles, the other Lost Boys are barely forms — blurs of shadow and bear-fur and the faintest glints of eyes in the darkness, depth-lost in heavy crosshatched undergrowth that builds dimensional blackness without flat fills.

The jungle floor is in Midnight Blue shadow with Crocodile Shadow grey-green underpainting bleeding through the earth tones. The fern fronds overhead break into the panel composition from both sides, their edges dissolving into the organic irregular border. The light shaft is cold silver-white, making Tootles' face the single warm note — Lost Boy Earth in his worn patched hide clothing, his earnest skin catching that one bright column as though the moon chose him specifically and then forgot why.

The half-width panel composition presses in from both sides — jungle density creating a tight visual corridor with Tootles at its mouth, small and determined, the other boys dissolved into the dark mass behind him.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Tootles: The most gentle and most unfortunate of the Lost Boys — slightly smaller than the others, with a face permanently set to the expression of someone who has just missed something wonderful by a moment. His eyes are large and earnest, the most visibly kind eyes in the Neverland ensemble. He carries himself with the quiet, undemanding humility of someone who has made peace with perpetual bad luck and found that the peace is real. Most tattered of all Lost Boy attire — patched animal-skin clothing in earth tones, honest wear accumulated rather than adventure trophies. Bear-skin hood drawn up. Color palette: Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914), Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) as underpainting undertone.

Art style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac pen linework, organic and gestural for Neverland. Crosshatching builds dimensional shadow. Rich ink-heavy darks. Slightly irregular organic panel borders. The too-vivid oversaturated quality of a cherished memory.
