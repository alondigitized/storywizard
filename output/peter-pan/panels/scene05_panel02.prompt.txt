Ground-level low-angle shot pushing through dense Neverland jungle at night — the camera sits almost at fern-height, looking up and forward along a narrow path of dark undergrowth. Massive luminous watercolor ferns crowd both edges of the frame, their fronds rendered in deep Neverland Viridian and near-black, crosshatched with dimensional shadow that builds a tunnel of living darkness. The dominant palette is Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) and murky Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240), the jungle pressing in from both sides with the patient weight of something watching.

Tootles fills the foreground center — slightly below mid-frame, advancing toward the viewer, bear-skin hood drawn close around his face. His small hands grip a dagger in front of his chest in both fists, the blade pointed upward, held with the reverence of prayer rather than the readiness of combat. His face is caught in a single shaft of descending moonlight — the only illumination in the panel, strikingly bright against surrounding darkness, almost overexposed, the quality of a memory returned to until it glows too vividly. His expression is one of concentrated, unguarded hope: large earnest eyes slightly raised toward whatever waits ahead, mouth set with quiet, undemanding determination. He is not brave from swagger — he is brave from want.

Behind him, the other Lost Boys are rendered as dense blurs of shadow and bear-fur — indistinct silhouettes pressed into the Midnight Blue darkness, their shapes suggested by crosshatching and massed watercolor shadow rather than defined linework. They are darkness with breath. Only Tootles exists in full clarity, the moonlight selecting him alone from the group.

The jungle floor is alive with bioluminescent detail — faint phosphorescent fungi at the panel edges, a soft silver-green glow beneath the fern fronds catching the Midnight Blue undergrowth. Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) underlies the entire composition as a ground wash, the murky grey-green of still swamp water and ticking time. Panel borders are organic and slightly irregular, the Neverland convention — fern fronds breach the left border, a shadow vine interrupts the lower right corner.

The moonlight shaft on Tootles' face is too perfect, too isolated — the light of a scene that has been remembered rather than lived, saturated beyond realism in the manner of childhood memory inflating what time diminishes.

His eyes are large and earnest, the most visibly kind eyes in the Neverland ensemble. He carries himself with the quiet, undemanding humility of someone who has made peace with perpetual bad luck and found that the peace is real. The most tattered of all Lost Boy attire — patched animal-skin clothing in earth tones, honest wear rather than adventure trophies. Color palette: Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914), Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240).

ART STYLE — Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration with soft luminous watercolor washes and delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Rich atmospheric backgrounds with meticulous natural detail. Characters with simple expressive faces and large emotive eyes. The overall feeling is nostalgic wonder — childhood adventure painted with the beauty and melancholy of a world you can never quite return to.
