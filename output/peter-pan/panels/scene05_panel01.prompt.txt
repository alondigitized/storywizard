Style: Edwardian ink-and-wash with luminous watercolor washes — detailed, expressive pen linework inspired by Arthur Rackham and Edmund Dulac, combined with the cinematic storytelling sensibility of contemporary graphic novels. Lines are organic, gestural, and slightly irregular in Neverland (alive, dreamed, never quite stable), and precise, architectural, and controlled in London (orderly, safe, constrained). The overall aesthetic is a 'dark fairy tale': beautiful and slightly unsettling, where childhood wonder and genuine menace coexist in every image. Shadows are rich and ink-heavy; light sources are warm, golden, and magical. Crosshatching is used to build dimensional shadow rather than flat fills. The visual grammar shifts perceptibly between the two worlds: London panels have harder outlines and more formal compositions; Neverland panels breathe with softer borders and looser, more dynamic mark-making. The whole adaption should read as a storybook being remembered rather than one being told for the first time — every Neverland image carries the slight oversaturation and too-vivid quality of a cherished memory.

His eyes are large and earnest, the most visibly kind eyes in the Neverland ensemble. He carries himself with the quiet, undemanding humility of someone who has made peace with perpetual bad luck and found that the peace is real. SCENE: Extreme bird's-eye aerial view of Neverland at night — memory itself looking down from an impossible height. Dead center: a lightless black lagoon, reflecting nothing, the eye of the composition. Around it, four concentric arcs wind through jungle and shadow like the marks of a living clock. Innermost: the Lost Boys crouching through bioluminescent undergrowth, daggers drawn, patched bear-skins in Lost Boy Earth warmth — Tootles at the outer edge of their cluster, slightly separated, eyes wide. Behind them: the pirate column threading darker trees, torches bobbing amber — Smee's round, incongruously cheerful form distinct among taller figures, striped cap catching firelight. Third arc: near-invisible Piccaninny silhouettes ghosting through blue-black shadow — Tiger Lily at the rear, spine erect, feathered headdress the single composed vertical in the ring's motion. Outermost: only eyes — paired glints of cold light padding through absolute darkness, the great beasts present only as watching. No figure faces backward. The whole island reads as a clock face of appetite winding itself without hands. Palette: Midnight Blue (#0D1B4B) sky pressing down; Neverland Viridian (#1A7A5E) pulsing from the innermost jungle; Lost Boy Earth (#8B6914) warming the torchlight column; Crocodile Shadow (#4A5240) pooling in the lagoon and the beast-dark. Neverland scale exaggeration in full effect — trees fractionally too vast, sky fractionally too high. Panel borders organic and irregular, foliage interrupting the frame edge. The image carries the eerie, golden-green beauty of a beloved nightmare: oversaturated, too alive, too perfect to be real.
