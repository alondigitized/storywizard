IMAGE PROMPT:
Bird's-eye aerial view of Neverland at night, rendered as a vast clock-face composition centered on a black mirror lagoon that reflects no stars. Four concentric arcs of movement radiate outward from this dark center through jungle and shadow, each ring defined by its own distinct light and depth. INNERMOST RING: the Lost Boys crouching through luminous undergrowth, their rough bear-skin silhouettes lit from below by bioluminescent fungi and phosphorescent moss — warm Lost Boy Earth ochres glowing against deep Neverland Viridian shadow, daggers barely catching the faint undergrowth light. SECOND RING: a pirate column winding through darker canopy, bobbing torches casting hard amber pools that illuminate fragments of crimson coats and steel — Hook's Crimson bleeding through Midnight Blue forest. THIRD RING: near-invisible Redskin silhouettes ghosting through blue-black shadow, defined only by the negative space they carve in the treeline — no torches, no glow, only the suggestion of deliberate, disciplined movement. OUTERMOST RING: absolute darkness except for paired eyes — great beasts padding through the island's farthest shadow, their eyes catching some unknown light source in fractional golden-green flashes. No creature looks behind it. The entire composition rotates clockwise, every ring pursuing the one ahead, forming an unbroken circle of appetite. The black lagoon at the center is perfectly still — the eye of this living clock, reflecting nothing.

The island's color is wrong in the way memory is wrong: too green, too saturated, the Neverland Viridian pressed past what nature permits, the torchlight amber bleeding into jungle gold at the edges. The sky above is deep Midnight Blue shot through with silver, stars too large and too numerous, the whole island floating in an indigo void like something preserved in glass. Scattered across the forest canopy: bioluminescent points of phosphorescent silver-green, tiny Crocodile Shadow undertones in the water's edge suggesting the lagoon's deeper, older darkness.

The panel format is full-width, panel borders organic and slightly irregular — a branch intrudes at one corner, a wave-edge at another — the Neverland visual grammar of borders that the island itself refuses to respect.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — The Lost Boys group includes Tootles: slightly smaller than the others, placed at the compositional edge of the innermost ring, his large earnest eyes just visible in the undergrowth glow, his patched animal-skin clothing in warm Lost Boy Earth ochres accumulating honest wear rather than trophies. Tiger Lily's people form the third ring: her silhouette placed at the rear of the war-party in the position of greatest honor, head erect even as shadow, her ceremonial ochre-and-umber palette a warm ghost in the blue-black. Smee is a compact, rounded figure in the pirate column, his striped knit cap glimpsed between torches, a domestic roundness incongruous among taller pirates, rendered in London Slate with Lost Boy Earth undertones.

Studio Ghibli-inspired watercolor illustration — soft luminous watercolor washes with delicate hand-drawn linework reminiscent of Hayao Miyazaki and Kazuo Oga. Rich atmospheric backgrounds, meticulous natural detail, deep indigos and silvers for night. The world feels alive, layered, handpainted — nostalgic wonder painted with the beauty and melancholy of a world you can never quite return to.
