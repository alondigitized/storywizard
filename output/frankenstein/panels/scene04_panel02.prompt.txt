Extreme medium close-up, tight on two pairs of hands filling the frame as moral counterpoint. Victor Frankenstein's living hands — long-fingered, scholar's hands of striking elegance — grip and slowly rotate a disembodied human forearm and hand specimen, holding it the way a watchmaker examines a mechanism. Victor's hands are Stage 2 Ingolstadt obsession: beautiful in structure but deeply filthy, nails rimmed black with grime, tendons visible along the back of the hand, faint yellow-green corpse tint bleeding into the skin suggesting galvanic contamination. The specimen — a severed forearm and hand — is rendered with unflinching 18th-century anatomical illustration precision: every tendon strand crosshatched into visibility, every mottled bruise of putrefaction a different grey-green register, the skin pulling at the fingertips, the waxy pallor of stopped circulation. The composition rhymes the two hands — living above, dead below — as explicit moral counterpoint, the elegant and the decayed in intimate contact.

A battered leather notebook lies open at the lower edge of frame, its pages dense with tight handwriting and anatomical sketches rendered in suggestive ink strokes — legible as dense notation and detailed biological diagrams but containing no readable language.

Single light source: a lantern positioned directly above, casting pure Amber Candlelight (#D4882A) in a tight cone downward onto the hands and the open notebook. The light is warm, almost tender — jarringly so given the subject. Everything beyond the circle of lamplight dissolves absolutely into Storm Violet-Black (#1A0F1E) — no ambient light, no background detail, pure consuming darkness. The hands appear to float in void.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism fusing Gustave Doré's engraving gravitas with Mike Mignola's chiaroscuro mastery. Heavy crosshatching creates texture dense as 18th-century anatomical illustration — the specimen rendered with the obsessive line-work of a Vesalius plate, Victor's living hands with slightly looser, warmer hatching that distinguishes living from dead flesh. Selective amber watercolor wash bleeds across Victor's knuckles and the lamplight pool; the specimen is rendered almost entirely in ink with minimal color — Charnel Ash Grey (#A0A0A0) and faint Corpse Yellow-Green (#C5C46A) for the decay tones.

Emotional register: clinical detachment masking profound transgression — the composed, almost beautiful stillness of a man who has ceased to feel revulsion. The warmth of the lamplight is not comfort; it is the light of obsession, the last warmth in a man becoming cold. The image should evoke the sensation of witnessing something intimate and terrible — a private ritual the viewer was not meant to see.
