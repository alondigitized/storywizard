IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme close-up panel, low Dutch angle framing. The composition is consumed entirely by Victor Frankenstein's two hands suspended inches above the partially assembled female form — hovering in space, fingers splayed wide, trembling, neither advancing nor retreating. The hands are the image's absolute center and moral weight.

Victor's hands rendered as a surgeon's hands gone wrong: long, elegant scholar's fingers, knuckles sharply defined, tendons raised beneath pale skin. At this stage of deterioration — haggard, Stage 3 to 4 — the hands carry visible trembling in the splayed tension of each finger. The blue-grey veins beneath the skin are visible. These are the same hands that assembled something they cannot now unmake. Rolled shirtsleeves expose pale forearms, a detail of terrible intimacy and labor.

Beneath the hovering hands: the female form rendered in deliberate abstraction. Not a body — a suggestion of one. Pale assembled curves of material and flesh, anatomically evocative without being explicit. One slack hand lies partially visible at the edge of frame, fingers not yet fully formed — the knuckles smooth, unresolved, two digits fused where individuation was never completed. The surface of the form carries the visual quality of wet clay and old parchment, slightly waxy, deeply wrong.

A watercolor wash of Corpse Yellow-Green (#C5C46A) bleeds across the female form in uneven pools — the color of galvanism and transgression, the color of life-that-should-not-be, applied unevenly as though the wrongness is seeping from within. This is the dominant color note of the form itself.

The shadow cast by Victor's hovering hands falls across the female form in sharp, precise chiaroscuro — the shadow geometry echoes iron bars, a cage laid across something imprisoned. This shadow is rendered with Mignola-weight ink blacks, clean and absolute.

A single candle occupies the far right edge of frame, its flame bent sideways in an unseen draft — the only light source in the composition, warm Amber Candlelight (#D4882A) that barely holds back the Midnight Prussian Blue (#1B2A4A) darkness pressing from every edge. The candle's warmth reaches the hands but fails to illuminate the full form beneath them — the body remains half-dark, half-light, unfinished in every visual sense.

Heavy crosshatched inking throughout — the texture of 18th-century anatomical illustration turned to horror purposes. The background dissolves into abstracted chiaroscuro shadow rather than architectural detail; this is the psychology of the moment, not the room. The Orkney darkness presses in from all edges.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism — Gustave Doré engraving precision fused with Mike Mignola ink mastery. Expressionist watercolor washes of yellow-green and deep blue bleed beyond the ink lines suggesting emotional contamination. Anatomical illustration heritage in the rendering of the hands. German Expressionist shadow geometry. No text, lettering, or symbols of any kind.
