Style: Dark Romantic Expressionism — a fusion of Gustave Doré's engraving gravitas, Mike Mignola's chiaroscuro ink mastery, and loose expressionist watercolor washes. Heavy crosshatched inking creates texture reminiscent of 18th-century woodblock prints and anatomical illustration, while selective watercolor bleeds suggest emotional states rather than literal reality. Figures are rendered with anatomical precision in the Gothic tradition — slightly elongated, subtly distorted under psychological stress — while backgrounds oscillate between hyper-detailed architectural environments and abstracted, emotionally expressive voids. The visual language draws from German Expressionist cinema (Nosferatu, The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari) for its dramatic use of shadow, diagonal composition, and the distortion of space to mirror psychological states. Every panel should feel as though it could exist as a steel engraving in a 19th-century illustrated edition — precise, dark, morally weighted — but animated with raw emotional urgency. The human body is the primary subject: faces must carry the full burden of guilt, grief, longing, and horror that the text demands. Backgrounds yield to psychology.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Victor Frankenstein: Victor begins the novel as a figure of almost classical Romantic beauty — the idealized young hero. High cheekbones, dark expressive eyes full of feverish light, thick dark hair swept back from a high forehead. His features are sharp and fine, his bearing that of a distinguished family who has never doubted its own gifts. As the narrative progresses, his face becomes the story's primary emotional canvas: hollowed cheeks deepen into permanent shadow, the feverish eyes acquire dark rings, the mouth compresses into a line of guilt. By the Arctic scenes he is skeletal — a gaunt, driven revenant of his former self, aged beyond his years, his beauty consumed by what he has made and what he cannot unmake. Victor should be drawn on a slightly smaller page-scale than the Creature, emphasizing their inverted power dynamic.; Early scenes (Geneva, Ingolstadt): high-collared white shirt, long dark coat with brass buttons, waistcoat, cravat — the dress of a well-born gentleman-scholar of the 1790s, impeccably kept. As his obsession grows, clothing deteriorates: coat stained, cravat undone, waistcoat unbuttoned, hair uncombed. In laboratory sequences he works in shirtsleeves rolled to the elbow — the only figure in the novel to ever appear undressed for labor, the civilized man reduced to something rawer. In the Arctic pursuit he wears heavy furs over deteriorated clothing, the elegant scholar buried beneath survival necessity.; Eyes: large, dark, intensely expressive — they shift from luminous idealism to haunted guilt to feverish obsession across the full narrative arc and must be drawn with maximum care in every close-up, Hands: always visually prominent — elegant scholar's hands that are also the hands that assembled the Creature; they tremble in scenes of guilt and shake with fever in later chapters, A single streak of silver appears at his left temple after William's death, spreading progressively through his dark hair across subsequent scenes as an objective timeline of grief, Progressive physical deterioration used as a visual timeline: readers should be able to roughly date any scene by the depth of shadow beneath his eyes and the degree of gauntness in his cheeks, In mirror or reflective surfaces — window glass, still water, polished metal — his reflection may occasionally carry a faint resemblance to the Creature's silhouette: the visual emblem of their doubled nature; color palette: Midnight Prussian Blue — his dominant color, always present in his coat and the ambient shadows that gather around him, Amber Candlelight — present only in early domestic scenes with family and Clerval, progressively fading from his palette as the novel advances, Corpse Yellow-Green — bleeds into his skin tone during laboratory sequences and fever episodes, marking the Creature's contamination of his flesh, Ice White — in the Arctic scenes, as he becomes a figure whose distinction from death grows increasingly ambiguous.

COMPOSITION: Overhead medium shot looking down from above the ship's rail — a steep, vertiginous angle that reduces Victor to a suspended figure caught between the Arctic void below and the ship's dark timber above. Three thick rope lines plunge diagonally across the frame in harsh expressionist geometry, converging and crossing like prison bars, caging his limp body within their architecture of strain. The German Expressionist diagonal is absolute: nothing in the frame is horizontal.

CHARACTERS: Victor hangs in a rope sling, body entirely limp — arms loose, head tipped back to face upward directly toward the viewer as though falling into the sky. His long dark coat is sodden and rigid with ice, its brass buttons glazed with frost, the fabric stiffened into something architectural and terrible. His face is skeletal — Stage 5 deterioration — cheekbones pressing against pale Ice White skin like knuckles through a glove, the hollows of his cheeks deep enough to hold shadow permanently. Dark eyes open wide, fixed on nothing, burning with internal fever the cold has failed to extinguish. Ice crystals crust his thick dark hair, his brows, the set of his jaw. A silver streak catches Arctic light at his left temple. His elegant scholar's hands hang open, trembling even in unconsciousness. Three sailors at the ropes above him — rough, broad, red-faced, straining with muscular effort — rendered in thick vital ink, bewildered expressions exchanged above the strange freight they haul. They are solid, present, of this world; Victor is not.

ENVIRONMENT: Arctic — cold frontal light, flat and unforgiving, bleaching all warmth. The ship's dark timber rail cuts across the upper frame, barnacled and ice-rimed. Below Victor, glimpsed through the rope geometry, the Arctic sea is a strip of midnight Prussian blue fractured with ice-white floe. The sky beyond is the same toneless grey as bone. No warmth anywhere in the palette — Ice White, midnight blue, charcoal grey, the faint corpse yellow-green bleeding into the shadows pooled in Victor's hollow cheeks. The rope lines are rendered in dense crosshatched ink that gives them weight and threat. The sailors cast strong downward shadows; Victor casts almost none — a man whose substance is already departing.

EMOTION: The shock of encountering something that should not have survived — a figure simultaneously alive and consumed, beautiful and destroyed, pulled from the void but not quite returned to the world. Awe, pity, and the cold certainty that whatever this man has endured has not yet finished with him.
