Wide horizontal panel, fever-dream nightmare sequence. Organic, hand-drawn irregular border with softly dissolving edges signals the dream state.

**Composition:** Low camera angle, slightly below street level, looking up along a gently curving Genevese cobblestone street. The street bends subtly upward at the horizon as though the earth itself is nauseated, cobblestones elongating and stretching like pulled taffy toward the edges of the frame. Arched stone doorways and limestone facades line the street, rendered in precise Doré-style crosshatching at center, dissolving into loose expressionist ink wash at the periphery — architecture becoming suggestion, solidity becoming doubt.

**Characters:** Elizabeth Lavenza occupies center-frame, facing the viewer directly. Her golden hair falls loose over her shoulders, catching the silver moonlight in luminous waves. She wears a white muslin dress, softly glowing — simultaneously bridal and spectral in the cold light. Her expression is radiant, alive, joyful — cloudless blue-grey eyes wide open and warmly lit, as though she is unaware of everything wrong. She is the warmest, most solidly rendered element in the panel. Victor Frankenstein stands directly behind her, his dark coat visible at her shoulders, arms wrapped around her from behind in an embrace that reads as both tender and desperate. His face presses close to hers in the gesture of a first kiss — but his large dark eyes are wide open, staring forward over her shoulder with pure terror. His expression is feverish, Stage 2 deterioration — still handsome but with the feverish hollowing of obsession beginning. His dark hair is slightly disheveled. His visible hands, gripping Elizabeth's arms, are elegant but white-knuckled.

**Environment and Atmosphere:** The dominant palette is silver-white moonlight and deep indigo (#1B2A4A) shadow. The distortion intensifies toward the panel edges: stones stretch, arches lean at impossible angles, a doorway at far left has no discernible depth. The street curves upward at the horizon into darkness.

**Lighting:** Cold, near-frontal moonlight falls on Elizabeth, giving her a near-halo quality — light from above and behind per the hope/memory convention. Victor receives side-lighting: half his face in deep indigo shadow, one eye and cheekbone silver-lit. The single candle-gold glow at the vanishing point pulls the eye forward into dread.

**Style:** Dark Romantic Expressionism. Doré engraving precision at center dissolving into expressionist watercolor wash at the dream-edges. Mignola-depth chiaroscuro in the shadows. German Expressionist spatial distortion — diagonal compositional tension, warped geometry. Heavy crosshatching in stone textures blooms organically into the surrounding darkness. The image evokes grief rehearsing itself before the dreamer is awake enough to stop it.
