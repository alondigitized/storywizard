Extreme tight close-up portrait, Victor Frankenstein's face in severe three-quarter angle, filling the panel. A single lantern source bisects him with surgical precision along a near-vertical line: the illuminated half rendered in luminous detail, the shadow half swallowed in absolute darkness with only the faintest gleam of one eye visible deep within the black.

Victor at Stage 2 deteriorating toward Stage 3 — still classically handsome but visibly consumed: high cheekbones beginning to jut sharply, dark circles pooling beneath his large, intensely expressive dark eyes like old bruises pressed into pale skin. His thick dark hair falls uncombed across his high forehead. Lips are parted, slightly, not in gasping horror but in the terrible stillness of total, monstrous concentration — a man who has seen the edge of something forbidden and has chosen to step closer. His eyes are wide and absolutely dry, fixed at something beyond the frame. He has not blinked in too long.

Expressionist distortion, subtle but deliberate: the shadow side of his face is fractionally elongated, the geometry not quite trustworthy, as though the darkness is physically reshaping him. From the eye on the illuminated side, loose radiating ink lines extend outward like heat shimmer from hot iron — the visual language of a mind burning itself through. The overall effect is of a face becoming something other than what it was.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism fusing Gustave Doré's engraving gravitas with Mike Mignola's chiaroscuro mastery. Heavy crosshatching builds the illuminated skin with the texture of 18th-century anatomical illustration — precise, cold, obsessively rendered. The shadow side resists crosshatching entirely, becoming a near-total ink void with one eye as a pale, barely-resolved gleam. Selective watercolor wash bleeds emotionally outward from the shadow edge.

Color palette: the illuminated half carries Corpse Yellow-Green contaminating his skin tone — the galvanic taint of the charnel house soaking into his flesh, the laboratory's sickly light doing its work. Midnight Prussian Blue dominates the shadow half, cold and consuming. The lantern source throws a faint Amber Candlelight warmth that the surrounding darkness is actively extinguishing. The background, where it resolves at all, is Charnel Ash Grey — stone, shadow, barely-implied anatomy charts and architectural arches dissolving into darkness at the periphery.

Emotional register: beautiful and ruined simultaneously. This is the face of a young man in the act of crossing a threshold that cannot be uncrossed — not horrified, not ecstatic, but in the grip of something beyond both. The composition should evoke German Expressionist cinema: Nosferatu's geometry of shadow, the Cabinet of Dr. Caligari's distortion of the real. No natural light exists in this space. No natural light should be felt.
