Extreme tight close-up portrait of Victor Frankenstein, three-quarter angle, face filling nearly the entire frame. Stage 4 deterioration: gaunt hollowed cheeks carved into deep shadow, dark rings beneath haunted eyes, a single silver streak visible at his left temple cutting through disheveled dark hair. His large dark eyes are wide and glassy, pupils fully dilated — the expression of a man whose certainty has just collapsed inward. His jaw is clenched but the muscles at his temples visibly tremble. Sweat glistens in beads across his brow and upper lip. His gaze angles sharply downward and slightly left, focused on something below frame — but his eyes have lost their object, turned inward toward a horror no external scene could produce.

Primary light source: a single candle positioned below and slightly left of frame, casting severe theatrical under-lighting. Amber candlelight rakes upward across his chin, the underplane of his nose, the hollows of his eye sockets — every groove and hollow filled with deep shadow, every protrusion harshly lit. The under-lighting transforms his fine features into something ghoulish and self-condemning, the Gothic horror convention of guilt made visible on the face.

Heavy crosshatched ink work defines the shadow topography of his face — dense parallel hatching deepens the hollows beneath his cheekbones and brow until they approach pure black. The crosshatching has the quality of 18th-century engraving, each stroke deliberate and weighted. Corpse yellow-green bleeds as a faint ambient contamination into the deepest shadows beneath his jaw and at the corners of his eyes — not literal, but felt, the Creature's psychological stain seeping into his skin. His unbuttoned coat collar and loosened cravat suggest advanced disordered dress; only the edge of his shirt at his throat is visible.

Background is abstracted almost entirely into darkness — deep Midnight Prussian Blue and Storm Violet-Black dissolving behind him, no environmental detail legible. The composition is pure psychological portrait: the world has ceased to exist except as darkness pressing against his face.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism. Mike Mignola's chiaroscuro ink mastery fused with the anatomical precision of Gustave Doré — the face rendered with absolute fidelity to bone structure and musculature, then distorted by shadow into expressionist horror. The weight of Romantic guilt made physical. German Expressionist cinema lighting: Nosferatu's under-lit faces, the geometry of shadow as moral revelation. Ink-forward with minimal watercolor wash — only the amber of the single candle flame and the cold violet-black of the surrounding void.

Mood: the precise instant before action, when the thought arrives and has not yet become choice. Not theatrical horror — something more interior and more devastating. A man seeing his own reflection clearly for the first time. The image should feel like a steel engraving cut from a waking nightmare — precise, dark, irreversible.
