IMAGE PROMPT:
Full-bleed cinematic panel, no borders, bleeding to all page edges. Horizontal split composition unified by an unbroken sky.

LEFT HALF: Ground-level wide shot on a dirt road outside Geneva's city walls. Victor Frankenstein stands alone on the path, his back to us, facing away into indeterminate distance. He is seen entirely from behind — a dark silhouette against cold stone and grey sky. His long dark coat is pulled tight around his frame, collar raised. His posture is the visual language of unbearable weight: shoulders slightly forward, spine subtly curved, as though something invisible and crushing has been laid across his back. He is absolutely still. His dark hair is disheveled, a faint streak of silver visible at his left temple even from this angle. His hands are not visible — buried in his coat, pressed against his body. He does not move toward or away from anything. He simply exists in the aftermath.

Behind him, Geneva's city walls rise massive and dark — enormous blocks of dressed stone rendered in dense crosshatched ink, the architecture oppressive in scale, dwarfing the solitary figure. The gate is closed. The walls offer no warmth, no aperture, no sympathy. They are simply there, enormous and indifferent.

RIGHT HALF: The dirt path continues faintly, then dissolves. No architecture. No figure. Only the vast, flat grey sky — continuing unbroken from the left half, the same overcast vault pressing down across the full width of the panel. The horizon is barely distinguishable from the air above it. The right half is near-empty: distance, atmosphere, and an unbearable quiet. A suggestion of rooftops far beyond the walls, barely resolved, barely present.

The two halves are not separated by a gutter or border — they are joined by the unbroken sky, making Victor and the unseen act simultaneous, occupying the same world, the same held breath of a moment.

ATMOSPHERE AND LIGHT: Flat, cold, overcast light — the Arctic frontal light that reveals everything and forgives nothing. No shadows cast with warmth. The light is grey and total and pitiless. Color palette dominated by Charnel Ash Grey (#6E6E6E / #A0A0A0) for sky, stone, and earth. Midnight Prussian Blue (#1B2A4A) bleeds into Victor's coat and the deepest shadow at the base of the walls. The faintest trace of Corpse Yellow-Green taints the shadows immediately surrounding his figure — a colorist's whisper, not a spotlight. No amber. No warmth anywhere in the panel.

ART STYLE: Dark Romantic Expressionism. Doré's engraving weight in the stone architecture — dense crosshatching building the walls into something almost geological. Mignola's ink discipline in Victor's silhouette — a single resolved dark shape against grey. Expressionist watercolor wash for the sky — loose, flat, emotionally evacuated. The horizon between earth and sky should be imprecise, the two zones bleeding into each other. The panel should feel vast, cold, and utterly silent.
