IMAGE PROMPT:
Tall, narrow vertical composition shaped like a standing coffin, cinematic low-angle view looking up along the scaffold's wooden framework from below and slightly behind. Dark Romantic Expressionism: Gustave Doré steel-engraving gravitas fused with Mike Mignola chiaroscuro ink mastery and loose expressionist watercolor washes.

COMPOSITION: The scaffold dominates the frame from bottom to top, its heavy timber uprights and crossbeam forming a near-architectural gateway that dwarfs the human figures completely. Justine stands at the topmost platform, her back entirely to the viewer, a small dark shape against the vast pale sky. The vertical lines of the scaffold's uprights repeat and echo the coffin format of the panel itself — architecture and composition locked in grim formal agreement. Below, the crowd is rendered as a single undifferentiated dark mass, featureless, threatening in its anonymity. A robed priest stands slightly to Justine's left, head bowed, his silhouette hunched and turned inward.

CHARACTERS: Justine wears her plain dark servant's dress with white collar — orderly, composed, impeccably neat even here. Her slight figure is erect. Her head is tilted fractionally upward, toward the sky, not toward the crowd — a gesture of refusal, of looking past everything earthly. Her composure reads from the back alone: the set of her shoulders, the stillness of her hands at her sides, the quality of someone who has decided what she is. The priest's bowed head rhymes with her raised one: his surrender to the moment, her refusal of it.

ENVIRONMENT: Pre-dawn exterior. The sky is a vast milk-white expanse of dense, fine crosshatching — every mark deliberate, the sky itself rendered as a steel engraving, cold and unyielding, a sky that feels printed rather than lived beneath. At the horizon, barely visible where the distant rooftops of Geneva interrupt the lower frame, the white bleeds into a faint, bruised yellow-grey — the color of a candle that has nearly gone out, not warmth but the memory of warmth. No sun. No cloud with beauty. Only the machinery of dawn without mercy.

STYLE AND COLOR: Heavy Doré-style crosshatching throughout the scaffold's timber structure, ink lines dense and architectural. The crowd below is pure Mignola: solid black masses with no interior detail, shadow as physical weight. Charnel Ash Grey (#6E6E6E) governs all tones — timber, stone cobblestones below, the priest's robes. Ice White (#EAE6D6) for the sky, slightly warm-tinted, not pure. Justine's white collar catches the pale pre-dawn light as the sole small point of Ice White against dark fabric. The bruised horizon: a faint wash of dying amber-grey, #D4882A drained to near-nothing, the color temperature of a world losing its last warmth. Expressionist watercolor bleeds softly at the sky's edges — the engraving texture and the wash coexisting, the precision of judgment and the dissolution of grief. Cold, flat, frontal pre-dawn light. No shadows with warmth. No mercy in the light.

EMOTION: Solitude magnified to the scale of the sky. The terrible composure of the innocent who have run out of world to appeal to.
