Slim horizontal letterbox panel, extreme widescreen crop compressing sky onto earth. VICTOR FRANKENSTEIN walks alone on a diagonal mountain road that cuts from the lower-left corner toward the upper-right, his body angled hard against driving wind. He wears a dark coat with brass buttons, rain-darkened and wind-whipped open at the hem, cravat loose, hair disheveled — a silver streak clearly visible at his left temple. His cheeks are hollowed, his dark eyes wide and turned slightly outward as though registering something the conscious mind refuses, mouth a compressed line of guilt and dread. Stage 4 deterioration: unmistakably handsome but consumed.

A single lightning bolt tears through the upper-right quadrant in corpse yellow-green, the only illumination. It strikes the scene from above and catches the wet road stones below Victor's feet, throwing reflected upward light across his lower face — his jaw and throat lit from beneath in the horror convention, his brow and eye sockets falling into shadow. The cold flash renders the pale road, fractured rock face, and the exposed planes of his coat in partial white against surrounding darkness.

Wind-bent pines line both sides of the road, their trunks leaning inward over Victor at accusatory angles, branches clawing toward each other overhead like closing hands. The road surface is slick with rain — fragmented reflections of the yellow-green lightning shimmer in the standing water at the path's edge. The Jura mountains rise on the right, their mass dissolved into the sky, mountain and storm the same churning violet-black and ash-grey, all horizon erased. The letterbox format physically crushes the sky downward, making the world feel airless, the path endless, escape impossible.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism fusing Gustave Doré engraving gravitas with Mike Mignola chiaroscuro ink mastery. Heavy crosshatching textures the road stones and mountainside with 18th-century woodblock print quality. Loose expressionist watercolor washes bleed the storm's violet-black into the rock, deliberately dissolving the boundary between geology and weather. Victor's figure is anatomically precise but slightly elongated under psychological stress, every line of his posture reading as a body moving toward something it cannot bear to face.

Color: Storm Violet-Black (#1A0F1E / #2D1A3D) dominates sky, mountains, and treeline shadow. Corpse Yellow-Green (#C5C46A) for the lightning bolt and its wet reflections. Midnight Prussian Blue (#1B2A4A) in the deep treeline. Charnel Ash Grey for road surface and fractured stone. Zero warmth — amber is entirely absent. A subtle yellow-green tint bleeds into the shadows pooling around Victor's feet, the Creature's psychological contamination made ambient in the atmosphere itself.
