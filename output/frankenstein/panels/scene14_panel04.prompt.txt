Full-bleed borderless panel, no panel borders, no frame. A 19th-century bridal chamber rendered as a Romantic deathbed painting in dark ink and cold watercolor washes — evoking Henry Wallis and John Everett Millais filtered through Gustave Doré's crosshatched gravitas and expressionist chiaroscuro.

COMPOSITION: Wide cinematic frame, horizontal. The bed dominates the center and right two-thirds of the composition, elevated slightly, draped in white. A tall window occupies the far right background — wide open, its heavy curtains billowing inward like a shroud in slow motion, admitting a flood of cold moonlight. Victor stands at the far left edge, framed in the doorway, hand still pressed to the doorframe.

ELIZABETH: Sprawled across the bed in terrible, painterly stillness. White bridal gown with period-accurate high waist and delicate embroidery spread around her in soft folds — simultaneously wedding dress and burial shroud. Thick golden hair fanned across white bedclothes in disordered waves, catching cold moonlight. Dark bruising at her throat — the marks of strangulation, rendered precisely but not gratuitously. Head fallen at a slight unnatural angle, half-covered by her own golden hair. One bare arm trails from the bed's edge toward the stone floor, fingers slightly open. Eyes closed. Expression eerily peaceful — which is the source of the horror. Rendered with exquisite anatomical care: she looks as though she is asleep.

VICTOR: Left edge of panel, Stage 4–5 physical deterioration — haggard, hollowed cheeks, dark rings beneath eyes, the silver streak visible at his left temple. Long dark coat with brass buttons, cravat loose. Body completely rigid, frozen mid-step — one hand pressed white-knuckled to the doorframe, the other open at his side, empty. His flintlock pistol caught in mid-fall beside his foot — a motion blur, the only kinetic element in a perfectly still room. Mouth fractionally open, unable to form sound. His figure is rendered slightly elongated, subtly wrong in proportion — psychological distortion. His shadow falls back behind him into the dark corridor, reaching away from the room, fleeing without him.

ENVIRONMENT: Stone-floored chamber. Impeccably neat except for one overturned vase of white flowers beside the bed, water spreading silently across the dark stone floor in a slow cold pool. The moonlight pours through the billowing curtains as a watercolor bleed of cold blue-silver across white — not a shaft of light but an atmosphere, saturating the room. All warmth is absent: amber has been completely extinguished. Dominant palette is Ice White, Midnight Prussian Blue, and Charnel Ash Grey, with a breath of Storm Violet-Black in the deep architectural shadows. The door behind Victor is darkness absolute.

MOOD AND STYLE: The room feels airless, suspended. Heavy crosshatched inking defines Elizabeth's figure and the architectural details; cold blue-silver watercolor washes bleed outward from the window. The composition mirrors Romantic deathbed iconography — geometric, deliberate, almost reverential in its horror. No warmth anywhere. Light sources: moonlight only, frontal and cold, revealing everything, forgiving nothing.
