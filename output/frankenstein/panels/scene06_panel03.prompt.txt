IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme tight close-up, graphic novel panel. Only Victor Frankenstein's eyes, the bridge of his nose, and the uppermost arc of his sweat-drenched brow fill the entire frame — his face presses flush against all four borders with no breathing room, no negative space, no escape. The composition is deliberately claustrophobic, suffocating; there is nowhere for the eye to retreat.

Victor is in Stage 3 of his deterioration — post-creation breakdown. His dark hair is plastered to his forehead in sweat-soaked strands. The skin across his brow and the bridge of his nose is rendered in fine stippling: individual beads of cold perspiration catching what little light exists, each droplet precisely placed in the tradition of 19th-century steel engraving. His eyebrows are drawn upward in pure involuntary terror, creasing deep lines into the forehead above.

His eyes are the entire subject of the panel. They are blown open beyond normal human range — pupils dilated to enormous black discs, consuming nearly the full iris, the whites showing in a full ring around each eye: classic scleral show, the physiological signature of extreme shock. The eyes of a man whose body has reacted to nightmare before his mind has caught up. Within the dark iris of one eye — the left, slightly more prominent in the composition — a single trembling point of warm amber-gold is reflected: the flame of an unseen candle somewhere beyond the panel's edge. This tiny warm point, approximately the size of a pinhead within the iris, is the only warm color in the entire image. Everything else is cold.

The governing palette is Midnight Prussian Blue (#1B2A4A) and Storm Violet-Black (#1A0F1E) — the shadows are deep, nearly absolute, pressing in from every direction. The skin reads in cold Charnel Ash Grey (#A0A0A0), blue-tinted with the chill of the room and the residue of nightmare. The single amber candlelight reflection (#D4882A) in the iris operates as an isolated point of heat in an otherwise arctic composition — the warm world just barely, tenuously present.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism. The crosshatching over the skin is dense and precise in the manner of Gustave Doré's engraving technique, with finer stippling reserved for the sweat beads and the subtle texture of the lower eyelids. Mike Mignola's chiaroscuro influence governs the absolute blacks pressing in from the edges — the darkness does not graduate, it cuts. Watercolor wash in cold Prussian blue bleeds unevenly through the shadows, soft-edged and organic against the hard ink lines. The pupils are rendered as pure flat black with no detail — voids rather than eyes.

The emotional register is pure wrenching terror at the threshold of waking: this is a man whose body knows it has survived something his mind has not yet processed. The image should feel like the inside of a scream that has not yet found its voice.
