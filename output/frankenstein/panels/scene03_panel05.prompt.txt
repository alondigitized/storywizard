Extreme close-up, narrow horizontal strip panel — a claustrophobic cinematic sliver of image containing nothing but two hands and dark wood. Composed as a tight widescreen crop, the frame cuts off all context above and below. No background, no environment, no suggestion of space beyond the desk's edge.

Two young man's hands grip the front lip of a heavy wooden lecture desk with white-knuckled intensity. The hands are elegant, long-fingered scholar's hands — fine bones visible beneath taut skin, the hands of a well-born gentleman not yet marked by labor. But in this moment they betray everything: knuckles blanched bone-white against the pressure, tendons raised in sharp relief along the backs of the hands like bridge cables under load, the grip of a man holding himself in place against an inner tide. The fingers curl under the desk's edge with a ferocity that suggests the wood might splinter.

The desk surface fills the lower half of the panel — old hardwood rendered in meticulous fine crosshatching, every grain line precise and deliberate, ink work that evokes 18th-century anatomical illustration and Gustave Doré engravings. The wood is dark, warm-toned, worn smooth by decades of students — a surface of institutional permanence against which this private, convulsive grip reads as transgression.

From above, a thin watercolor wash of cold blue-white light — the ghost of intellectual electricity — falls across the knuckles and the back of the hands. The wash is thin, luminous, not warm: it traces the highest points of the knuckles in ice-pale blue-white (#EAE6D6 with blue undertone), leaving the valleys between tendons in deep Prussian shadow (#1B2A4A). The light source is directional and cold, carrying no comfort.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism — Mike Mignola-weight black ink, heavy confident crosshatching on wood grain and skin shadow, expressionist watercolor wash applied loosely over precise linework. The tonal range is extreme: near-black shadow, brilliant knuckle-white, nothing in between that softens the tension. Color palette stripped to its essentials: deep Prussian blue shadow, bone-white stress points, dark amber-brown wood grain. No warmth in the light; all warmth is in the wood, and the hands are fighting it.

The image evokes physical compulsion — the body announcing what the mind has not yet admitted. Something irrevocable is beginning.
