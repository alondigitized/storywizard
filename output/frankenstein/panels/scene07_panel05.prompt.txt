Extreme close-up portrait, Dutch angle slightly tilted, Victor Frankenstein's face filling the frame — direct frontal confrontation with the viewer, eyes locked forward, breaking the fourth wall with the force of recognition rather than invitation. He is Stage 4 in his deterioration arc: cheekbones carved deep, dark hollows beneath the eyes, the first silver streak newly appearing at his left temple in the dark hair. A man who was once beautiful, now consumed.

Both hands are raised just into the lower frame — elegant scholar's hands, slightly trembling, fingers splayed and curved as though deflecting something invisible. The hands are slightly blurred, caught in instinctive, aborted warding-off motion.

His expression is the specific horror of confirmation: eyes stretched wide, pupils contracted to pinpoints — not by lamplight but by a lightning flash already fading. The wide-open eyes carry recognition rather than surprise, as though the worst thing he ever feared has simply arrived wearing the face he always knew it would. Mouth fractionally parted, the lower lip dropped no more than a breath's worth — a sound begun and swallowed before it could become a scream.

The crosshatching around his eyes and jaw is at maximum density — expressionistic hatching that functions architecturally, carving the face into near-skull territory. The shadow under his cheekbones is absolute; the shadows at his temples press inward. His skin is almost bone-white with a faint sick tinge of Corpse Yellow-Green bleeding into the shadowed hollows — the Creature's contamination visible in his own flesh.

Lighting from below and to one side: a dying lightning afterimage throws its last cold Corpse Yellow-Green wash upward across his face from beneath, theatrical and unholy, turning the familiar horror convention into something specific and earned. The other half of his face is already surrendering to Storm Violet-Black shadow — the darkness closing back in.

Behind him, the storm sky: the deep bruised purple-black of Storm Violet-Black (#1A0F1E / #2D1A3D), the lightning afterglow fading from the upper corners of the frame in spectral yellow-green, leaving only darkness and the suggestion of churning cloud rendered in loose expressionist ink-wash. The Alpine wilderness is not present — only sky, storm, the last light dying.

No lantern in frame — already fallen, already forgotten.

Art style: Mike Mignola chiaroscuro ink mastery combined with Gustave Doré engraving density. Heavy crosshatched ink lines define the face with the precision of an anatomical study. The background transitions from crosshatched ink to near-abstract watercolor wash — the world dissolving as Victor's certainty solidifies into dread. German Expressionist cinema framing: the composition itself is slightly wrong, the angle slightly too close, refusing distance or comfort. The face must carry everything.
