Wide establishing shot from a high, receding vantage point deep within the charnel house vault. Victor Frankenstein stands at absolute center, rendered impossibly small against the vast stone interior — a single dark vertical figure swallowed by architecture. His back is fully turned to the viewer. His arms hang straight at his sides, hands open and still. He has set down the specimen; the table beside him holds the dark suggestion of it, half-resolved in shadow. He does not move. The stillness of his posture is not rest — it is the stillness of a decision made and sealed.

The vault rises in massive Gothic arches above him, the stone ribs curving overhead in a configuration that reads unmistakably as the interior of a skull — expressionistically distorted, the vaulted ceiling bowing inward at angles that Euclidean geometry would not permit. Crosshatched ink renders the stonework in dense, engraver's detail: every crack, every shadow-pooled recess, every anatomical chart pinned to the far walls barely visible at this distance. The negative space of the vault is enormous. Victor occupies perhaps one-tenth of the vertical composition.

The lantern on the floor beside him is the sole light source. It throws amber upward in a tight radius — a warm circle that ends abruptly, consumed by black. The amber does not comfort. It isolates. The Amber Candlelight color (#D4882A) pools around his boots and catches the hem of his dark coat, but it stops before it reaches the walls, which recede into Charnel Ash Grey and then absolute Storm Violet-Black. No gold remains in this image — the warmth has curdled to amber and the amber is ringed by black.

Victor is in Ingolstadt obsession mode — Stage 2 deterioration: high-collared shirt visible beneath his dark coat, the cravat loosened, coat slightly disheveled. Even from this distance his silhouette reads as a young man becoming something driven: shoulders slightly forward despite the stillness, head neither bowed nor raised — perfectly level, looking at nothing, resolved. His dark hair is just visible at the collar.

The compositional frame is a vignette — the edges of the image dissolve into white space, the vault's darkness fading to nothing at the borders as though this moment exists outside of time, isolated on the page. The architecture curves most dramatically at the top of the frame, the stone arching like the dome of a skull pressed down over him.

Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism fusing Gustave Doré's engraving gravitas and Mike Mignola's absolute chiaroscuro. Crosshatching dense and directional. Watercolor washes of amber pooling at center, bleeding to grey, then violet-black at the periphery. Perspective subtly warped — walls lean, the geometry untrustworthy, space expanding beyond what physics should allow. The emotional register: sublime dread, irreversible resolve, the loneliness of a man who has just chosen to play God.
