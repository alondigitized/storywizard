IMAGE PROMPT:
Wide establishing shot, low angle slightly elevated to reveal the full stone-vaulted workshop interior. The composition echoes a Gustave Doré engraving — architecturally precise, geometrically ordered, but radiating existential dread from every shadow. Low barrel-vaulted ceiling of rough-cut Scottish stone presses down on the space. A single arched window dominates the far wall, rain streaking the glass in diagonal silver lines, the storm beyond a wall of black-violet darkness pressing inward.

Center-frame: Victor Frankenstein hunches over a long stone worktable, his back three-quarters to the viewer, shoulders curved inward and upward as though bracing against an invisible blow. He is at Stage 4–5 deterioration — gaunt, almost skeletal, shirtsleeves rolled to the elbows, his dark coat abandoned somewhere in shadow. A single silver streak catches candlelight at his left temple. His silhouette is angular, haunted, the posture of a man who has not slept in days.

On the table before him, barely resolved in shadow: a half-assembled female form, draped with linen that falls in heavy folds, only the outline unmistakable — curved shoulder, the suggestion of a face turned away, one limp hand at the table's edge. The form is more absence than presence, more silhouette than body. Surgical instruments are scattered across the table — bone saws, forceps, curved needles — each one catching a precise gleam of candlelight, each one looking both medical and obscene in this context.

Two thick tallow candles provide the only illumination, placed on the table near Victor's hands. The candlelight pools in amber circles that barely penetrate the surrounding dark. Light source is from below and in front of Victor, casting his face — what little we can see in three-quarter view — in dramatic underlighting: hollow cheeks carved into deeper shadow, the orbital bones of his skull becoming visible, the hands prominent and trembling at the table's edge.

The stone walls are rendered in dense Mike Mignola crosshatching — thick ink, deep black, the mortar lines suggesting something ancient and indifferent. Anatomical drawings pinned to the far wall are barely visible, dissolving into shadow at their edges. The floor is wet stone, reflecting the candlelight in broken amber smears.

Color palette: Midnight Prussian Blue dominates the entire space — walls, ceiling, storm beyond the window. Amber Candlelight is precious and insufficient, fighting a losing battle against the dark. Charnel Ash Grey for the stone surfaces and the linen draping the form. Subtle Corpse Yellow-Green bleeds into the ambient shadows around Victor's hands and the worktable — the contaminating color of transgression. Storm Violet-Black beyond the arched window, alive with compressed fury.

Watercolor washes applied loosely over the crosshatched ink, the colors bleeding at their edges as though the scene itself is unstable. The composition should feel as though the darkness is actively closing inward from all four corners. No warmth survives here. Art style: Dark Romantic Expressionism, steel-engraving precision, Gothic sublime.
