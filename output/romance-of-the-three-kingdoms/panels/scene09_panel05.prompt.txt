Wide-angle exterior shot, lower-register panel composition. A packed-earth courtyard before a yamen gatehouse — the architecture is Han Dynasty post-and-beam, its upturned eaves just barely suggested at the panel's upper edge in loose, atmospheric ink-wash brushwork. The sky above is vast, pale, and nearly empty: Parchment Ivory fading to cold Bone White, carrying the emotional weight of a world that has stopped watching.

Dominant focal point: a government inspector lashed with rope to a thick wooden hitching post, center-left of frame. His official court robes are torn at the shoulder and chest, hanging open in disheveled folds of deep purple and Smoke Ash. Around his neck on a cord hangs his own rank seal — heavy, brass-fitted, worn like a criminal's placard. His posture is total collapse: torso slumped forward against the rope's resistance, knees buckled, head drooping. Rendered in merciless detail — the specific broken geometry of a man stripped of the authority that held him upright.

To the left and slightly behind the post, Chang Fei: compact, volcanic, low center of gravity. Bullet-round head, large protuberant eyes wide with righteous fury beneath bristling moustache. Earth umber military garb, worn and battle-roughened. His right arm is extended in a full mid-arc swing — a long willow switch caught at the moment of fracture, its upper third already splintering outward in ink-streak motion lines. Volcanic orange-red highlight catches the underside of his arm. He is not triumphant. He is delivering a verdict.

To the right of the panel, three white-haired village elders stand as witnesses. Their robes are plain, worn, the Parchment Ivory of ordinary age. Their faces carry the specific exhaustion of grief fully spent — not vindication, not anger. One elder cups both hands over his mouth, eyes closed. A second bows his head, chin nearly to chest. The third simply watches, hollow-eyed, his hands loose at his sides. They are people the world already broke, watching the world get corrected too late.

Ink-wash wuxia realism throughout. Foreground figures — Chang Fei, the inspector — rendered in dense, muscular linework with full meticulous detail. The elders in slightly softer linework with atmospheric wash. The yamen walls and sky in nearly abstract, brushed negative space — a pale emptiness that amplifies rather than fills the scene's weight.

Color temperature reads cool and desaturated: Earth Umber for Chang Fei, Smoke Ash across the inspector's fallen robes, Parchment Ivory in elder garments and open sky. A single warm highlight — the orange-red beneath the fracturing switch at moment of impact — the only heat in the frame.

Hard-edged shadow geometry falls from the hitching post across the packed earth, corruption's shadow cast by institutional architecture. The overall mood: not catharsis. Grief witnessed. Justice too long delayed to feel like victory.
