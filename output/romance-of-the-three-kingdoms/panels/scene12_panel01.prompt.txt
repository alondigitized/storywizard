IMAGE PROMPT:
Full-bleed borderless panel. Extreme low-angle wide shot from ground level looking steeply upward at the Imperial Han Palace gates — the viewer is small, the gate is god-sized. The composition is vertical and overwhelming: the architecture dominates the upper two-thirds of the frame, the flood of soldiers fills the lower third.

The gate itself is the protagonist. Massive lacquered double doors in deep vermillion — each door the height of ten men — have been shattered inward, their ancient timber split and burning. The doors are rendered in meticulous, reverent classical linework: every bracket arm, every carved ridge beast along the sweeping upturned roofline, every painted beam visible in precise architectural draftsmanship — and then systematically consumed by loose, explosive ink-wash strokes of fire. The contrast between the precise and the destroyed is the panel's central tension. Ornamental ridge beasts crumble from the eaves and fall as glowing embers. A carved dragon above the gate arch splits down its center, its halves falling away from each other in a diagonal symmetry that mirrors the breach below.

Fire erupts in roaring vertical columns of Cinnabar Red (#C1392B) and deep amber-orange, their cores bone white with heat, their edges dissolving into billowing black smoke. Smoke rolls upward in thick ink-wash masses, flattening against a night sky turned the color of blood and ash — the sky itself is Cinnabar Red fading to Midnight Ink (#1A1A2E) at the frame's uppermost edge, as though heaven is burning from below.

Through the shattered breach, Yuan Shu's armored soldiers pour like a tide. In the immediate foreground plane, two or three soldiers are rendered in full detail — lacquered armor, lowered spears, helmets catching orange firelight — silhouetted dark against the inferno behind them. In the middle distance, figures become detailed shadow-forms, their spear points and banner poles catching the light as individual bright accents. In the far distance, the army is pure ink-wash mass, an abstract surge of Midnight Ink and Cinnabar Red, thousands rendered as a single dark tide. Banners catch the fire: dark crimson against the orange columns, their fabric torn and billowing.

The single dominant light source is the fire — warm orange-red, directional, casting hard geometric shadows from the architectural elements and long, dramatic shadows from the soldiers across the foreground ground plane. No other light source exists. The world has been reduced to fire and dark.

The overall composition deliberately echoes a Song Dynasty landscape scroll turned catastrophic: the palace occupies the painting's upper registers with the monumental authority of a mountain; the soldiers are as small against it as travelers on a classical scroll path. The building is dying magnificently. Every figure is dwarfed by the architecture they are destroying.

Art style: ink-wash wuxia realism, shuǐmò technique for fire and smoke, precise linework for architectural elements, explosive diagonal mark-making for flames, cinematic chiaroscuro, mythic scale. No text, no lettering, no symbols.
