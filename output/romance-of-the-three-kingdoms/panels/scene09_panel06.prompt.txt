IMAGE PROMPT:
Narrow vertical portrait panel, ink-wash wuxia realism. Late afternoon exterior — the gate of a Han-era administrative post, weathered timber and stone posts framing the scene. Long diagonal shadows stretch across packed-earth ground.

COMPOSITION: Camera placed just inside the gate, looking outward. Liu Pei occupies the lower-center of the frame, small against the enormous sky — his figure roughly one-fifth the panel's height, positioned where the gate threshold meets the open road. He walks directly away from the viewer, already past us, already gone. The vast ink-wash sky fills the upper three-fifths of the panel, bleeding from pale Imperial Gold at the horizon to deep storm-grey at the top edge — atmospheric, atmospheric, restrained. Generous negative space dominates. The panel breathes.

CHARACTERS:
Liu Pei — seen entirely from behind, mid-distance, diminishing. Worn muted blue-green traveling robes, the fabric hem showing road dust and old mending. His characteristic long arms visible even from behind, hands falling past his hips. His sword sheathed at his side — no hand near the hilt. His posture is rigidly straight, shoulders neither proud nor collapsed — the specific geometry of a man carrying a decision that has already settled into bone. He does not turn. His ears, long-lobed, faintly visible at profile edge. No triumph in his silhouette. Only forward.

Chang Fei — blurred and smaller in the middle background, several paces behind Liu Pei and to his left, just inside the gate threshold. Compact and dense, bullet-round head, earth umber military garb. He holds the remains of a broken willow switch — a short, ragged length of it — at his side, loosely, the fury that drove it entirely spent. His body language reads as a spent storm. His protuberant eyes are downcast, the explosive energy collapsed inward. He follows because following is all there is now.

The Inspector — barely more than a brushstroke at the far right background, very small, hitched to the post. Figurally insignificant. Already irrelevant to this composition. He exists only to prove the panel has moved past him.

ENVIRONMENT: The gate posts frame the shot with their weathered wood grain. Beyond: a wide, flat road receding into distance, flanked by spare dry vegetation. The sky is the true environment — an ink-wash expanse in the Song Dynasty tradition, rendered in graduated washes: pale Imperial Gold warm at the horizon, rising through Smoke Ash to deep cool grey. Long afternoon shadows cut diagonally across the earth, Midnight Ink against Parchment Ivory dust. The light source is low, golden, almost gone.

STYLE: Ink-wash wuxia realism. Most restrained brushwork in the sequence — sparse, deliberate, elegiac. Soft diffused outdoor shadows. Thin clean panel border. The atmospheric depth of a classical Chinese scroll. Emotional register: not tragedy but something quieter — the specific loneliness of a righteous choice already made and already irreversible.
