IMAGE PROMPT:
Extreme close-up impact panel, graphic novel page. Near-abstract composition dominated by a single massive diagonal: Chang Fei's eighteen-foot spear shaft slices the frame from lower-left to upper-right, its iron tip erupted through the center of Teng Mou's chest armor. The spear violates the panel border at both edges.

TENG MOU — Yellow Turban rebel soldier, mid-rank, stocky build, round face, wearing battered ochre-yellow battle armor over a saffron-yellow underrobe. His yellow headband has been knocked loose and spirals upward and off-frame, a trailing ribbon of Yellow Ochre (#D4943A) against darkness. His body forms a sharp, almost architectural backward arc — torso wrenched away from us, spine bent to its limit, one arm thrown wide, legs lifting from the saddle. His face is turned toward the viewer in three-quarter view: eyes wide, mouth slightly parted — not screaming, not in agony, but seized by pure astonishment, the expression of a man who has just understood something enormous and has no time to process it. The classical shuǐmò figure of a man surrendering entirely to physics.

At the spear shaft, only Chang Fei's gauntleted fist is visible — earth-umber leather, iron-riveted knuckles, gripping the shaft with absolute finality. No arm, no face, no body. Anonymous. Instrumental. Inevitable.

BLOOD AND LIGHT: The wound-point at the center of Teng Mou's armor is the sole luminous element in the panel — a starburst of Cinnabar Red (#C1392B) radiating outward in ink-splash patterns. Not gore but calligraphy: the blood moves as brushstrokes, deliberate and controlled, as though a master calligrapher marked the moment. From this bright center, darkness pools in every direction. The spear shaft, the surrounding armor, the horse's flank glimpsed at the bottom edge — everything drowns in deep chiaroscuro shadow using Midnight Ink (#1A1A2E) and Earth Umber. The wound is the only window of light in the composition.

STYLE: Traditional Chinese shuǐmò ink-wash technique fused with Western graphic novel draftsmanship. The horse and battlefield background are pure atmospheric suggestion — loose ink-wash, near-abstract, ochre dust and black smoke rendered as brushstroke masses with zero detail. The characters occupy the sharp foreground plane of crisp, muscular linework. The spear shaft has a calligraphic quality — one confident brushstroke from edge to edge. The backward arc of Teng Mou's body draws from classical Chinese figure painting: the body as a character in its own narrative.

COMPOSITION: Diagonal geometry almost entirely fills the frame. Negative space appears only in the deep shadows and the spiral of the departing yellow headband. Panel border is heavy and active — fractured where the spear tip exits frame. Color palette severely restricted: Yellow Ochre, Cinnabar Red, Earth Umber, Midnight Ink, with Bone White (#E8DFD0) only at the wound-burst epicenter. The overall register: monumental, elegiac, and absolutely still despite depicting maximum violence.
