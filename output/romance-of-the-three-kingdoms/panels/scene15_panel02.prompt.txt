Style: Ink-wash wuxia realism — a hybrid of traditional Chinese shuǐmò (water-ink) painting and cinematic Western graphic novel draftsmanship. Characters are rendered with meticulous, muscular linework in the tradition of classical Chinese figure painting, set against atmospheric, loosely-brushed backgrounds that echo Song Dynasty landscape scrolls. Dramatic chiaroscuro lighting carves heroes from shadow. Battle scenes employ explosive diagonal compositions with motion-blur ink streaks; intimate scenes use sparse, deliberate brushwork with deliberate negative space. The overall register is monumental and mythic — every panel should feel as though it could hang as a scroll painting, yet move with the kinetic urgency of a sword stroke. Think the visual vocabulary of Jae-Hwan Kim's 'King of Hell' fused with the atmospheric depth of Hokusai's wave compositions and the moral weight of classical Chinese court painting.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Ho Chin: The tragic figure of institutional mediocrity raised to catastrophic power. A heavy, well-dressed man who has mistaken the trappings of authority for authority itself. He should look exactly like what he is: a butcher's son in a general's robes, who has acquired the costume but not the character of power. His face carries vanity and anxiety in equal measure — always slightly worried about what people think of him, always slightly wrong about what he should be worried about. His physical bearing contrasts sharply with every true warrior in the story.; The most elaborate court robes in the story — layers of official silk that mark his proximity to the throne. The quality of his clothing should be the inverse of his character: the better he is dressed, the more clearly his emptiness shows. Colors of court legitimacy — deep purple, official crimson — worn without the moral substance to fill them.; Official court robes of excessive elaboration — the costume of power worn by someone who borrowed it, The expression of a man who cannot hear warnings because he is too busy deciding not to hear them, Physical softness in contrast to every warrior who surrounds him, The fatal pause — his indecision should be visible in his body language in every scene; color palette: Deep Purple — false court authority, borrowed legitimacy, Soft Crimson — imperial proximity without imperial virtue, Smoke Ash (#7A7A8A) — his moral ambiguity, his drift toward catastrophe

COMPOSITION: Medium-wide interior shot, low angle looking slightly upward toward Ho Chin seated on a lacquered reading dais at center-frame. He is placed at the lower-center third of the panel, deliberately dwarfed by the soaring chamber architecture above and around him. The vertical grandeur of the room — sweeping lacquered columns rising into shadowed ceiling beams — diminishes rather than elevates him. An open silk-bound memorial scroll drapes across his knees.

FIGURE: Ho Chin, heavy and soft-bodied, reclines in self-satisfied repose. Layers of deep purple and soft crimson official silk robes pool luxuriously around him, their elaborate embroidery reading as borrowed costume. His broad face tips downward toward the scroll, wearing the private, self-congratulatory smile of a vain man admiring his own reflection. Critically: the single amber oil lamp positioned low beside the dais casts upward-raking light — warmth pooling beneath his chin and cheekbones while deep shadows hollow his eye sockets, creating a skull-like quality beneath the smugness. His hands rest soft and unworked against the scroll's silk binding.

ENVIRONMENT: Han Dynasty palace interior, intimate private chamber. Hard-edged geometric shadows — corruption's architectural darkness — fall precisely between lacquered vermillion columns. Jade ornaments gleam on low tables; painted silk hangings cascade from unseen heights, their gold-and-crimson patterns fading into upper shadow. The chamber's wealth is suffocating rather than welcoming. In the far background corners, barely legible in the Midnight Ink shadows, two eunuch attendants stand motionless — professionally unreadable faces, elaborate robes blending into the dark spaces between columns, present like an omen he cannot see.

LIGHTING: Single warm amber lamp source, positioned below frame-center, casting upward raking shadows across Ho Chin's face. The upper two-thirds of the chamber recede into atmospheric ink-wash darkness. Hard shadow edges on all architectural elements; the eunuchs in the corners exist almost entirely in shadow.

PALETTE: Deep Purple (#4A235A) dominant in robes, Soft Crimson at robe borders, Smoke Ash in mid-ground architectural shadows, warm amber lamp glow concentrated on Ho Chin's face and scroll, Midnight Ink consuming the chamber's heights. No warm gold on Ho Chin — the Imperial Gold belongs to legitimate power; he has only its cheap imitation.

MOOD: Ironic grandeur. A small man in an enormous room, smiling at a document he is not wise enough to distrust. The beauty of the space is a trap he has mistaken for a throne.
