Style: Ink-wash wuxia realism — a hybrid of traditional Chinese shuǐmò (water-ink) painting and cinematic Western graphic novel draftsmanship. Characters are rendered with meticulous, muscular linework in the tradition of classical Chinese figure painting, set against atmospheric, loosely-brushed backgrounds that echo Song Dynasty landscape scrolls. Dramatic chiaroscuro lighting carves heroes from shadow. Battle scenes employ explosive diagonal compositions with motion-blur ink streaks; intimate scenes use sparse, deliberate brushwork with deliberate negative space. The overall register is monumental and mythic — every panel should feel as though it could hang as a scroll painting, yet move with the kinetic urgency of a sword stroke. Think the visual vocabulary of Jae-Hwan Kim's 'King of Hell' fused with the atmospheric depth of Hokusai's wave compositions and the moral weight of classical Chinese court painting.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Sun Chien: The Tiger of Wu — lithe, powerful, and radiant with the specific joy of someone who is exactly as brave as they need to be and knows it. Where Kuan Yu's heroism carries tragic weight and Chang Fei's carries explosive fury, Sun Chien's heroism is luminous and almost playful — a warrior who scales walls and seizes spears in mid-air because it is simply the correct thing to do and also, secretly, tremendous fun. Broad open face, clear and direct gaze, a build that communicates both speed and power without the heaviness of older fighters. He should look younger and more kinetic than the other heroes.; Lighter, more mobile armor than the northern heroes — the Wu-region aesthetic, designed for river warfare and rapid assault. Bright metal with minimal bulk, allowing the acrobatic movement that defines him. His color signature is a warm bronze-gold that catches light and movement simultaneously.; The acrobatic quality — even in still panels, his body reads as mid-motion, The broad, open face of someone who has never learned to dissemble, Youth and kinetic energy in contrast to the more weathered northern heroes, His signature feat — scaling walls — should define his spatial relationship to panel frames; color palette: Bronze Gold — martial glory, warmth, the south's more optimistic energy, River Blue — his eastern Wu origins, the Yangtze's power, Warm Silver — his armor, his blade, the flash of heroic action

COMPOSITION: Extreme wide cinematic frame, camera at ground level — the viewer is in the churned earth, looking up. Heavy border weight vibrating with impact energy. In the lower foreground, the rebel leader lies flat on his back in the dirt, armor askew, arms splayed, face turned upward toward the pale sky in stunned defeat. His horse is a blurred ochre streak exiting left frame. Center frame, Sun Chien is caught in the precise geometry of a perfect mount — left boot already seated in the stirrup, right leg swinging up and over, bronze-gold armor catching the flat summer light. His stolen spear extends left in a long diagonal; his sword catches silver light on the right. His posture is effortless, unhurried — a man mounting to ride on an ordinary morning. His broad face is turned slightly forward, not back. He does not look at what he has taken.

ENVIRONMENT: Summer battlefield, heat and dust. Yellow-ochre dust clouds billow from the churned ground. Around Sun Chien, the rebel cavalry line is fracturing — figures rendered as detailed silhouettes mid-scatter, left and right, the geometry of a broken formation. The background dissolves into dense, dark ink-wash abstraction: compressed strokes suggesting mass panic, motion-blur streaks of fleeing men and horses dissolving into an almost calligraphic chaos of Earth Umber and Smoke Ash. The sky above is bone-white and bleached — the flat overcast of high summer that gives no comfort.

LIGHTING: Single dominant source, flat overcast summer light from above — warm but diffused, casting almost no shadow on the chaos background. Sun Chien alone catches clean, warm bronze-gold light, as though the sun briefly broke cloud to illuminate him. The rebel leader below is in cool shadow, the earth cold and final beneath him.

MOOD: The still point in a smearing world. Sun Chien rendered in crisp, muscular linework — clean and certain amid the ink-wash dissolution of everything around him. The low angle makes him monumental; his ease makes him mythic. The rebel lord's upward gaze and Sun Chien's forward gaze never intersect — one man's story has ended; the other's has not yet registered that it has begun.
