IMAGE PROMPT:
Bird's-eye view, full-bleed borderless horizontal composition, looking straight down through an ocean of white-pink peach blossoms that fills the entire upper two-thirds of the frame. The blossoms are rendered in loose, wet shuǐmò brushwork — petals dissolving at their edges into pure negative space, ink bleeding into parchment ivory, pools of jade green showing through the canopy where branches part. The blossom mass has weight and depth, layered like a living ceiling.

Below the canopy, centered in the lower third, a stone altar glows amber-gold with open flame. Kneeling before it are three small figures — their silhouettes crisp and deliberate against the firelight. The tallest on the left, broad-shouldered with a flowing beard; the lean, still figure at center; the compact, low-set figure on the right — all three bowed in deep prostration, heads inclined toward the flame. The altar fire throws warm Cinnabar Red and Imperial Gold light upward, catching the rising columns of white incense smoke that spiral slowly into the blossom canopy above, dissolving into the petals.

Surrounding the altar in a vast open semicircle, three hundred figures hold torches. In the near distance, individual faces and robes are readable — earth umber, rough cloth, the honest faces of farmers and villagers. In the middle distance they become detailed silhouettes, torchlight catching the orange-gold of their flames. In the far distance the torches are pure points of warm light studded through the deep indigo dark, earthbound stars mirroring the sky above. The overall shape of the crowd forms a cupped bowl of light around the three kneeling brothers.

To the left of the altar, partially visible through the canopy shadow, a black ox and a white horse stand tethered to a peach tree. Their presence is quiet and monumental.

The sky behind and beyond the garden is ink-washed deep indigo — the specific blue-black of early spring dusk in Song Dynasty landscape painting. Along the horizon, a thin bleeding line of copper light — the last of the day — separates earth from sky. The transition from copper horizon to indigo zenith is gradual, almost geological.

Color temperature: warm jade green and parchment ivory dominate the blossoms. The altar and torches pulse with Imperial Gold and soft Cinnabar warmth. The sky deepens into Midnight Ink. The composition's emotional axis runs vertically — Heaven above watching through petals, Earth below arranged in reverence.

Art style: ink-wash wuxia realism blending shuǐmò atmospheric looseness in the blossom canopy with precise architectural rendering at the altar and firelit crowd. The three brothers rendered as crisp dark silhouettes — the still points of the composition. Dramatic chiaroscuro from below as the altar fire illuminates upward into the blossom ceiling. The entire frame should feel like a classical Chinese hanging scroll stretched into cinematic widescreen — sacred, vast, and luminous with the specific gravity of a moment Heaven has agreed to witness.
