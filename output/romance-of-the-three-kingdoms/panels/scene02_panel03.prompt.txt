Vertical panel composition — explosive energy surging upward from the lower-right frame edge, as though the panel itself cannot contain what has just entered it. Chang Fei erupts into the lower-right foreground, his bullet-round head thrust forward and upward, dominating the immediate space. He is built like a war drum — compact, dense, low center of gravity, shoulders wider than seems architecturally possible. His bullet-round head is nearly perfectly spherical, jowls bristling with coarse stubble, large protuberant eyes burning like black coals with impatient volcanic life, a pointed chin, and a bristling moustache that juts outward like a dare. He wears rough butcher's cloth in deep earth umber — practical, worn, unglamorous — forearms like carved oak emerging from rolled-up sleeves. He leans aggressively forward, one thick finger stabbing outward toward the left of frame — a physical challenge, a declaration, an invitation to war. His posture is a runaway horse mid-stride.

Liu Pei occupies the left side of the frame — still as a scroll painting, an almost eerie counterpoint. Tall and lean in worn blue-green traveling robes frayed at the hem, his wide-set eyes hold quiet watchfulness beneath their unusual placement. His face is composed to the point of inscrutability, rich red lips closed, arms of preternatural length at his sides. He faces Chang Fei with the stillness of a man who has been waiting for this exact moment his entire life and is careful not to show it.

The two figures create deliberate compositional contrast: Chang Fei's linework is thick, urgent, slashing — ink strokes that vibrate with compressed energy; Liu Pei's rendering uses finer, more deliberate brushwork, elegant negative space, the technique of classical Chinese figure painting. One crackling brushfire, one calligraphic scroll.

Background: a Han Dynasty market street rendered in loose atmospheric ink-wash — timber storefronts receding into soft mist, the architecture of a civilization going about its dying business. Warm amber lantern light falls from above-left, carving Chang Fei's broad face in dramatic chiaroscuro — earth umber in deep shadow, volcanic orange-red catching the ridge of his cheekbone and the whites of his intense eyes. Liu Pei receives the same light source but absorbs it differently — jade-ivory tones, cooler and more restrained.

Color palette anchored in Earth Umber and Jade Green, with warm amber light source providing the dominant warmth. Heavy ink-wash atmosphere in the background. A faint jade-green tonal wash bleeds into the space around Liu Pei — his moral signature color, barely perceptible. Panel borders are clean and thin — standard narrative scene. 8-head heroic proportion on both figures. No text, no lettering, no written characters anywhere in the composition.
