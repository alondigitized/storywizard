Extreme narrow vertical format, portrait orientation approximately 1:5 ratio — tall gutter panel, barely wider than a bookmark. Ink-wash shuǐmò technique executed with maximum restraint. Single focal object centered in the lower third of the composition: a yellow cloth turban, severed and collapsed in the dust, rendered in Yellow Ochre (#D4943A) with soft ink-wash brushwork. The fabric has lost its tension — it pools and crumples with the specific deflation of something that once held meaning and now holds nothing. Beneath the turban, a thin elongated line of dark ink — near-black, slightly pooled at one edge — spreads slowly across pale dust, suggesting liquid darkening earth without depicting anything explicit. The ground itself is rendered in the sparest possible terms: two or three dry-brush horizontal strokes of pale Earth Umber (#6B3D1E), leaving vast areas of Parchment Ivory (#F2EBD9) untouched. The negative space — the raw white of the page — constitutes seventy percent of the panel's surface area. This emptiness is not absence; it is the breathing room after catastrophe. In the uppermost fifth of the panel, barely materialized: three loose diagonal brushstrokes in diluted Smoke Ash (#7A7A8A) and Yellow Ochre, suggesting at the greatest possible distance a dust cloud rolling away from the viewer — the ghost of a broken army in retreat, rendered so lightly they might be imagination. No figures. No weapons. No sky detail. No horizon line. Lighting is ambient and diffuse, late afternoon haze, no visible light source — the illumination of aftermath rather than action. The overall technique references Song Dynasty ink monochrome scroll fragments: dry brush for texture, wet brush for the single pooled line, diluted wash for the distant dust. The emotional register is absolute stillness — the held breath after the last sword stroke. The turban's Yellow Ochre is the only saturated color in the panel; everything else approaches monochrome. The composition should read as a haiku made visual: one image, surrounded by silence, meaning everything the adjacent panels could not say directly.
