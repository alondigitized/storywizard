WIDE CINEMATIC SHOT, full-bleed borderless panel. Ink-wash wuxia realism — hybrid of traditional Chinese shuǐmò painting and Western graphic novel draftsmanship.

**Composition**: Three horizontal registers stack the frame. Upper two-thirds: the Han imperial palace consumed entirely by fire — sweeping post-and-beam architecture with upturned rooflines cascading in Cinnabar Red (#C1392B) and deep amber against absolute Midnight Ink sky. Smoke billows in dense ochre-grey masses, backlit from within by the blaze. Middle register: the north riverbank, a narrow strip of dark earth and sparse, firelit reeds. Lower third: the dark river, its surface alive with fractured, shimmering reflections of the inferno above — a mirror image of catastrophe broken into trembling fragments of orange, crimson, and bone-white by the current. The burning sky and its reflection trap the human figures between two versions of the same destruction.

**Foreground**: At the water's edge, centered and vertical — Chang Jang, senior palace eunuch. He wears only his heavy court robes of layered Midnight Ink black and tarnished imperial gold, richly embroidered fabric falling in deliberate folds, armor entirely absent. His back faces both viewer and soldiers — he faces the river, and its burning reflection. His posture communicates not collapse but finality: absolute stillness, a man who has made his last decision and stands inside it. Behind him, small and fragile against his bulk, cluster two young figures — the Emperor in frayed imperial gold-and-crimson robes, a child, and the young Prince beside him, their pale faces drawn in the firelight, eyes wide. They press close to Chang Jang as though he remains a wall between them and the world.

**Flanks**: From both sides of the frame, emerging from the burning-lit treeline — dark soldiers in silhouette, their torches small orange points closing inward through the undergrowth. They are rendered as detailed silhouettes in the middle distance, color-blocked in earth umber and fire-orange, the encircling ring made geometrically, inescapably visible.

**Lighting**: Single dominant source — the palace fire, warm orange-red falling across the entire scene from behind and above. Chang Jang's robes catch firelight on their upper surfaces; deep, hard-edged shadows fall downward in the corruption-geometric style. The river mirror repeats the same warmth in broken, restless fragments below. Soldiers' torches are secondary warm points against deeper flanking darkness.

**Atmosphere**: The deliberate stillness of a classical Chinese landscape scroll — one vertical figure at the horizon's edge, the horizontal black water before him, the burning sky above. Loose atmospheric ink-wash in the treeline and billowing smoke; sparse, deliberate brushwork in the near ground. The composition echoes a Song Dynasty scholar-at-the-shore painting translated into catastrophe — monumental, elegiac, the specific gravity of a man who has carried something as far as it can be carried.
