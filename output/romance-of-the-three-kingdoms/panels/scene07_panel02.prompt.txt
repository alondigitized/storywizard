Style: Ink-wash wuxia realism — a hybrid of traditional Chinese shuǐmò (water-ink) painting and cinematic Western graphic novel draftsmanship. Characters are rendered with meticulous, muscular linework in the tradition of classical Chinese figure painting, set against atmospheric, loosely-brushed backgrounds that echo Song Dynasty landscape scrolls. Dramatic chiaroscuro lighting carves heroes from shadow. Battle scenes employ explosive diagonal compositions with motion-blur ink streaks; intimate scenes use sparse, deliberate brushwork with deliberate negative space. The overall register is monumental and mythic — every panel should feel as though it could hang as a scroll painting, yet move with the kinetic urgency of a sword stroke. Think the visual vocabulary of Jae-Hwan Kim's 'King of Hell' fused with the atmospheric depth of Hokusai's wave compositions and the moral weight of classical Chinese court painting.

CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Chang Chio: The story's great mystic antagonist — a figure who began as a healer and became a revolution. His visual design must carry the weight of both genuine spiritual authority and the tragedy of a movement that burned too large and too fast. Elongated, almost ascetic figure — someone who has fasted and prayed and been transformed by it. Robes of yellow and deep teal that seem to move in winds other figures cannot feel. His eyes have a quality of looking at something vast and distant that ordinary mortals cannot perceive. His arms are partially raised — the gesture of an incantation — but descending, fingers loosening, the Storm Teal energy dissipating from his hands into fading wisps. His saffron-and-ochre robes and deep teal accents still billow in a wind of his own making, but the movement is disordered now, chaotic rather than commanded. The yellow headband blazes at his brow — a wound-bright band of Yellow Ochre against storm-grey fabric. A scroll case at his belt, partially visible. His rigid posture radiates the specific paralysis of a man whose certainty is disintegrating in real time.

The battlefield reads as abstract impressionism at distance: thousands of men reduced to panicked brushstrokes. MOOD: The stillness of a man watching his worldview shatter. Every line of his silhouette communicates arrested certainty. The panel should feel like the moment between the crack appearing and the collapse beginning.
