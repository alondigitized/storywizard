Style: Ink-wash wuxia realism — a hybrid of traditional Chinese shuǐmò (water-ink) painting and cinematic Western graphic novel draftsmanship. Characters are rendered with meticulous, muscular linework in the tradition of classical Chinese figure painting, set against atmospheric, loosely-brushed backgrounds that echo Song Dynasty landscape scrolls. Dramatic chiaroscuro lighting carves heroes from shadow. Battle scenes employ explosive diagonal compositions with motion-blur ink streaks; intimate scenes use sparse, deliberate brushwork with deliberate negative space. The overall register is monumental and mythic — every panel should feel as though it could hang as a scroll painting, yet move with the kinetic urgency of a sword stroke. Think the visual vocabulary of Jae-Hwan Kim's 'King of Hell' fused with the atmospheric depth of Hokusai's wave compositions and the moral weight of classical Chinese court painting.
CHARACTERS IN SCENE — Sun Chien: The Tiger of Wu — lithe, powerful, and radiant with the specific joy of someone who is exactly as brave as they need to be and knows it. Where Kuan Yu's heroism carries tragic weight and Chang Fei's carries explosive fury, Sun Chien's heroism is luminous and almost playful — a warrior who scales walls and seizes spears in mid-air because it is simply the correct thing to do and also, secretly, tremendous fun. Broad open face, clear and direct gaze, a build that communicates both speed and power without the heaviness of older fighters. He should look younger and more kinetic than the other heroes.; Lighter, more mobile armor than the northern heroes — the Wu-region aesthetic, designed for river warfare and rapid assault. Bright metal with minimal bulk, allowing the acrobatic movement that defines him. His color signature is a warm bronze-gold that catches light and movement simultaneously.; The acrobatic quality — even in still panels, his body reads as mid-motion, The broad, open face of someone who has never learned to dissemble, Youth and kinetic energy in contrast to the more weathered northern heroes, His signature feat — scaling walls — should define his spatial relationship to panel frames; color palette: Bronze Gold — martial glory, warmth, the south's more optimistic energy, River Blue — his eastern Wu origins, the Yangtze's power, Warm Silver — his armor, his blade, the flash of heroic action

COMPOSITION: Extreme wide panoramic establishing shot, squat and horizontal — aspect ratio approximately 3:1, anchoring low on the page like the final brushstroke of a scroll. Sun Chien occupies the dead center of the frame, rendered with full crisp linework, the one still point amid dissolution. His warhorse is fully airborne — all four hooves clear of the earth, mane streaming horizontal. Sun Chien's arms are thrown wide as wings: sword extended right, captured spear extended left, his bronze-gold armor blazing against the muted ochre battlefield. His broad face is turned three-quarters toward the viewer, expression luminous — not triumphant cruelty but the pure radiant clarity of someone who has just proven the world exactly as large as he imagined it.

On both flanks, rebel soldiers fracture and scatter outward like ink dropped in water, rendered as dissolving ink-wash silhouettes — loose, gestural, detail-stripped. They peel away from him in diagonal vectors, bodies angled in flight, weapons abandoned. The further from Sun Chien, the more abstract they become — middle distance figures as detailed silhouettes with yellow ochre headbands, far distance as pure ash-grey wash masses dissolving into atmospheric haze.

In the far background, the city wall of Wancheng rises as a massive horizontal band — tiny figures visible at its crenellations beginning their rope descent, rendered in precise miniature against the pale stone.

ENVIRONMENT: Battlefield in late morning clearing. The sky above has burned pale — heavy ochre-grey dust and dissipating smoke giving way upward to a high bone-white wash, luminous and vast. The ground is churned earth, yellow ochre and earth umber, scored with hoofprints and scattered weapons. Dust still hangs in long diagonal streaks behind Sun Chien's charge, trailing him like brushwork motion lines. The atmosphere is the specific exhausted clarity after chaos — the moment when the dust begins to settle and what happened becomes legible.

LIGHTING: Single dominant source — high morning sun, warm gold, striking Sun Chien from slightly above and ahead. His armor catches it as burning bronze-gold flares. Rebels fall into their own cast shadows as they flee. The city wall catches diffuse backlighting. The clearing sky creates a luminous negative space above the entire composition, the bone-white emptiness of a scroll painting where meaning breathes.

PALETTE: Restricted battlefield register — yellow ochre dust, earth umber ground, bone-white sky, warm bronze-gold hero, ash-grey dissolving rebels, pale stone wall. Cinnabar red only as the blood-edge accent on Sun Chien's blade. No warm palette in the fleeing figures — they desaturate as they flee.
